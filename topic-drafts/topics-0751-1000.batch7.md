## 811 — HER2-Positive Breast Cancer and ADCs

topicId: `645d7493-068c-4a62-b901-db00b7b802d9`

### Mentor intro

### Teaching points

## 812 — Hereditary GI Polyposis Syndromes

topicId: `20426d76-3951-4d18-a8cf-4a3ff38a04cd`

### Mentor intro
A handful of polyposis syndromes explain a small but preventable fraction of colorectal cancer, and they reward precise recognition: FAP and its attenuated form, MUTYH-associated polyposis, the hamartomatous syndromes (juvenile polyposis, Peutz-Jeghers), and the Lynch syndromes nearby. Genetic testing costs have collapsed but referral of high-risk patients lags â€” so the clinical skill is suspecting the syndrome from polyp burden, age and family history, then channelling the patient into surveillance or risk-reducing surgery that genuinely prevents cancer.

### Teaching points
- Hereditary adenomatous polyposis syndromes, including FAP and rarer variants, increase lifetime risk of colorectal and other cancers; updated European guidelines (38 experts) standardise their clinical management. [[PMID 38722804]]
- Inherited cancer syndromes are responsible for approximately 3-5% of all colorectal cancers, and although genetic-testing costs have plummeted, awareness and referral of high-risk patients remain the rate-limiting step. [[PMID 31705372]]
- Around 10% to 16% of colorectal cancer patients carry a pathogenic variant in a cancer-susceptibility gene, spanning a broader hereditary-colorectal-cancer syndrome landscape than polyposis alone. [[PMID 35577708]]
- Juvenile polyposis syndrome is a rare autosomal-dominant precancerous condition conferring increased gastrointestinal cancer risk, and should be suspected when hamartomatous polyposis features cluster in a family. [[PMID 35988962]]

### Uncertainties
- Optimal surveillance intervals and the threshold for prophylactic colectomy vary by syndrome, age and genotype, and are individualised in guideline practice.
- Chemoprevention (NSAIDs in FAP) modifies polyp burden but its effect on cancer endpoints is not definitive.

### MCQ angles
- FAP: hundreds of adenomas, APC gene, prophylactic colectomy, upper-GI surveillance.
- MUTYH-associated polyposis: autosomal recessive, attenuated phenotype.
- Hamartomatous syndromes: juvenile polyposis, Peutz-Jeghers (STK11) and their extracolonic cancers.
- When to refer for genetic testing: Amsterdam/Bethesda-style criteria and polyp-count thresholds.

### Case hooks

## 813 — Hereditary hemochromatosis diagnosis and phlebotomy

topicId: `91fb7e1c-bf01-43ed-b964-3fe7729b9efd`

### Mentor intro
Hereditary haemochromatosis is the commonest inherited disorder in northern European populations and one of the most satisfying to treat: the biochemical signature is elevated transferrin saturation, the gene is usually HFE C282Y, and venesection removes the excess iron before it destroys organs. The tragedy is underdiagnosis â€” arthropathy, diabetes, cirrhosis and cardiomyopathy are preventable when phlebotomy starts early, which is why unexplained fatigue with abnormal iron studies should always prompt the ferritin-and-transferrin-saturation pair.

### Teaching points
- Haemochromatosis is characterised by elevated transferrin saturation and progressive iron loading that mainly affects the liver; early diagnosis and treatment by phlebotomy can prevent cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, diabetes, arthropathy and other complications. [[PMID 35662478]]
- Hereditary haemochromatosis is one of the most common genetic disorders among persons of northern European descent, with recent advances in diagnosis, management and molecular diagnostic testing reflected in the ACG guideline. [[PMID 31335359]]
- Haemochromatosis is overdiagnosed in patients without true iron overload and underdiagnosed in many patients; early diagnosis by genetic testing and therapy are emphasised. [[PMID 37121243]]
- Most patients remain asymptomatic, but others develop advanced organ damage â€” arthropathy, diabetes â€” that reduces quality of life and survival, defining the treatment urgency. [[PMID 39928951]]
- Hereditary haemochromatosis is an autosomal-recessive disorder that disrupts iron homeostasis, causing systemic iron overload, and is the most common inherited disorder among people of northern European ancestry. [[PMID 34523883]]

### Uncertainties
- Penetrance of HFE C282Y homozygosity is incomplete â€” many carriers never develop iron overload, complicating screening policy.
- The role of hepcidin-pathway agents for patients unable to tolerate phlebotomy is investigational.

### MCQ angles
- Diagnostic sequence: transferrin saturation and ferritin first, HFE genotyping to confirm.
- Phlebotomy targets and maintenance regimens.
- Organ complications to screen for at diagnosis (liver, glucose, joints, heart, skin).
- Incomplete penetrance and who actually needs treatment.

### Case hooks

### Gaps

### Mentor intro

### Teaching points
- Pheochromocytomas/paragangliomas have a distinctive molecular landscape allowing assignment to clusters by genetic alteration; around 30-35% of Caucasian patients harbour germline mutations, underpinning personalised management. [[PMID 34147030]]
- Phaeochromocytomas are rare adrenal-medulla tumours that may be sporadic or part of a hereditary syndrome, and a considerable number carry germline or somatic mutations inherited in an autosomal-dominant pattern. [[PMID 31517632]]
- Neurofibromatosis type 1 is a complex autosomal-dominant disorder caused by germline NF1 tumour-suppressor mutations, with characteristic pigmentary features and a well-recognised phaeochromocytoma association. [[PMID 28230061]]
- Plasma or fractionated urine metanephrines are the biochemical test of choice for diagnosis, and the preoperative approach to phaeochromocytoma centres on alpha-blockade before any surgical manipulation. [[PMID 38849272]]

### Uncertainties

- SDH-mutation risk stratification, including for phenocopies such as SDHA and the paediatric surveillance question, is evolving.

- When to suspect and test for germline disease: young age, family history, multifocal or extra-adrenal disease.
- Biochemical diagnosis: plasma-free or urinary fractionated metanephrines.
- Preoperative management: alpha-blockade before beta-blockade, volume repletion.

## 815 — Hereditary Spherocytosis and Elliptocytosis

topicId: `fc7086d1-5ddd-4dba-beb7-20dc41f5f089`

### Mentor intro
Hereditary spherocytosis and elliptocytosis are disorders of the red-cell skeleton: defects in spectrin, ankyrin, band 3 or protein 4.2 weaken the membrane, splenic conditioning does the rest, and the result is a chronic haemolysis that announces itself through jaundice, gallstones and a positive family history. The laboratory signature is the spherocyte (or elliptocyte) on the film, and modern diagnosis mixes EMA-binding and osmotic-fragility studies with genetic testing.

### Teaching points
- Hereditary spherocytosis, elliptocytosis and stomatocytosis are inherited disorders caused by defects in various red-cell membrane proteins; the ICSH guideline standardises their laboratory diagnosis given heterogeneous clinical, biochemical and genetic presentations. [[PMID 25790109]]
- Membrane structural defects lead to hereditary spherocytosis and hereditary elliptocytosis, producing haemolysis; significant advances in diagnosis and clinical management of these inherited membrane disorders have been made. [[PMID 30504335]]
- Hereditary erythrocyte membrane disorders are caused by mutations in genes encoding transmembrane or cytoskeletal proteins, with decreased cell deformability and shortened erythrocyte survival as the main consequences. [[PMID 31364155]]
- Structural understanding of inherited red-cell membrane disorders explains altered cell function, reduced red-cell survival and the resulting haemolytic anaemia. [[PMID 28447420]]

### Uncertainties
- Thresholds for splenectomy â€” balancing haemolysis control against infection and thrombotic risk â€” are individualised and not standardised in the retained sources.
- Genotype-phenotype correlation remains imperfect, complicating prognostication from molecular results alone.

### MCQ angles
- Spherocytes on film: HS versus warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (DAT differentiates).
- Diagnostic tests: EMA binding, osmotic fragility, ektacytometry, genetics.
- Complications: gallstones, aplastic crises (parvovirus B19), splenomegaly.
- Elliptocytosis spectrum and hereditary pyropoikilocytosis.

### Case hooks

### Gaps

### Teaching points
- The European S2k guideline (updated from the 2015 S1) provides a decision aid for selecting, implementing and assessing appropriate therapy for hidradenitis suppurativa. [[PMID 39699926]]
- HS is a chronic inflammatory disease characterised by painful deep-seated nodules, abscesses and draining tunnels in the axillary, inguinal, genitoanal or inframammary areas. [[PMID 39862870]]
- HS is a chronic, debilitating inflammatory disease derived from the pilosebaceous unit affecting approximately 1% of the general population, with nodules, abscesses and tunnels as clinical hallmarks. [[PMID 37968174]]
- HS is a complex immune-mediated disorder initially presenting as folliculitis that can progress to advanced lesions such as fistulas requiring extensive surgical intervention. [[PMID 40530931]]

### Uncertainties
- Optimal sequencing of biologics and the role of surgery early versus late in tunnelling disease remain practice-dependent.
- Disease-modifying therapy that prevents progression, rather than suppressing flares, is not yet established.

### MCQ angles
- Hurley staging (I nodules/abscesses, II sinus tracts/scarring, III diffuse involvement).
- Treatment ladder: topical clindamycin -> tetracyclines -> anti-TNF biologics -> surgery.
- Comorbidity associations: obesity, smoking, metabolic syndrome, depression.
- Differentiating HS from boils and Crohn's-related perianal disease.

### Case hooks
- A 26-year-old with recurrent axillary abscesses and scarring is told she has recurrent boils; recognise HS and stage it.
- A patient with Hurley II disease fails antibiotic therapy; discuss biologic options using the guideline framework.

### Gaps
- Hurley-staging definitions, specific antibiotic/biologic regimens and dosing, and surgical technique selection are not covered by the retained abstracts; verify against the S2k full guideline before approval.
