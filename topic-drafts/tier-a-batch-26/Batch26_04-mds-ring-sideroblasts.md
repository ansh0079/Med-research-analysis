# MDS with Ring Sideroblasts: evidence record (batch 26, not published)

**Lead guideline:** Diagnosis and classification of myelodysplastic syndrome: International Working Group on Morphology of myelodysplastic syndrome (IWGM-MDS) consensus proposals for the definition and enumeration of myeloblasts and ring sideroblasts (2008), PMID 18838480, document id 6124. Read in full from the stored text.

## Synopsis (gist)

The spreadsheet lead is a mouse Sf3b1 knock-in paper and does not state clinical recommendations. This file uses the 2008 International Working Group on Morphology of MDS consensus on the definition and enumeration of myeloblasts and ring sideroblasts, which is the on-topic consensus stored in the database. It is a classification consensus, not a treatment protocol.

## All recommendations of the lead guideline (7 statements + 0 body-text details)

### IWGM-MDS consensus

1. It is recommended that (1) agranular or granular blast cells be defined (replacing the previous type I, II and III blasts), (2) dysplastic promyelocytes be distinguished from cytologically normal promyelocytes and from granular blast cells, (3) sufficient cells be counted to give a precise blast percentage, particularly at thresholds that are important for diagnosis or prognosis and (4) ring sideroblasts be defined as erythroblasts in which there are a minimum of 5 siderotic granules covering at least a third of the nuclear circumference.  
   *Anchor:* "It is recommended that (1) agranular or granular blast cells be defined (replacing the previous type I, II and III blasts), (2) dysplastic promyelocytes be distinguished from cytologically normal promyelocytes and from granular blast cells, (3) sufficient cells be counted to give a precise blast percentage, particularly at thresholds that are important for diagnosis or prognosis and (4) ring sideroblasts be defined"
2. Recommendations should be applied in the context of the WHO classification.  
   *Anchor:* "Recommendations should be applied in the context of the WHO classification."
3. After review- ing all the available bone marrow smears, the IWGM group recommended that myeloblasts in MDS should be classified as agranular or granular.  
   *Anchor:* "After review- ing all the available bone marrow smears, the IWGM group recommended that myeloblasts in MDS should be classified as agranular or granular."
4. These cells will thus include type II blasts as defined by FAB, as well as type III blasts as defined by Goasguen et al.12 Granular blasts must be distinguished from promye- locytes (see below).  
   *Anchor:* "These cells will thus include type II blasts as defined by FAB, as well as type III blasts as defined by Goasguen et al.12 Granular blasts must be distinguished from promye- locytes (see below)."
5. It should also be noted that when performing a mar- row dif ferential count, the myeloblast per centage should be determined by counting at least 500 nucleat- ed cells, with the total including at least 100 nonery- throid cells to improve precision.  
   *Anchor:* "It should also be noted that when performing a mar- row dif ferential count, the myeloblast per centage should be determined by counting at least 500 nucleat- ed cells, with the total including at least 100 nonery- throid cells to improve precision."
6. After a review of many cases of sideroblastic anemia, the group determined that ring sideroblasts should have at least 5 granules in a perinuclear distribution; that these granules could either surround the entire nucleus, be localized to portions of the perinuclear area or cover at least one third of the nucleus (Figures 3 and 4).  
   *Anchor:* "After a review of many cases of sideroblastic anemia, the group determined that ring sideroblasts should have at least 5 granules in a perinuclear distribution; that these granules could either surround the entire nucleus, be localized to portions of the perinuclear area or cover at least one third of the nucleus (Figures 3 and 4)."
7. The gr oup recommends that when counting ring sideroblasts all stages of erythroid precursors be counted and should include at least 100 nucleated erythroid pre- cursors and for the definition of RARS the required num- ber of ring sideroblasts remains at 15% as defined previ- ously in the FAB and WHO classifications.  
   *Anchor:* "The gr oup recommends that when counting ring sideroblasts all stages of erythroid precursors be counted and should include at least 100 nucleated erythroid pre- cursors and for the definition of RARS the required num- ber of ring sideroblasts remains at 15% as defined previ- ously in the FAB and WHO classifications."
