# Leishmaniasis: visceral vs cutaneous, kala-azar, liposomal amphotericin B: evidence record (batch 26, not published)

**Lead guideline:** Diagnosis and Treatment of Leishmaniasis: Clinical Practice Guidelines by the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) and the American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene (ASTMH) (2017), PMID 27927991, document id 6943. Read in full from the stored text.

## Synopsis (gist)

The stored text is the IDSA and ASTMH clinical practice guideline on diagnosis and treatment of leishmaniasis. Recommendations are phrased as "we recommend" or "we suggest" and cover specimen collection, use of more than one diagnostic method, species identification, antibody testing, who should be offered treatment for cutaneous disease, assessment of failure, inpatient monitoring, treatment of visceral disease, secondary prophylaxis, and testing around transplantation and tumour-necrosis-factor blockade. Doses that appear only inside tables are not reconstructed here.

## All recommendations of the lead guideline (16 statements + 0 body-text details)

### IDSA/ASTMH recommendations

1. We recommend the collection of tissue aspirates or biopsy specimens for smears, histopathology, parasite culture, and molecular testing [Strong, low].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend the collection of tissue aspirates or biopsy specimens for smears, histopathology, parasite culture, and molecular testing [Strong, low]."
2. We recommend using multiple diagnostic approaches to maximize the likelihood of a positive Leishmania result, using methods such as visualization of the characteristic amastigote in smears or tissue (histopathology); parasite isolation by in vitro culture; molecular detection of parasite DNA; and, for VL, serologic testing (see VI–VIII and Table 2).  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend using multiple diagnostic approaches to maximize the likelihood of a positive Leishmania result, using methods such as visualization of the characteristic amastigote in smears or tissue (histopathology); parasite isolation by in vitro culture; molecular detection of parasite DNA; and, for VL, serologic testing (see VI–VIII and Table 2)."
3. We recommend attempting parasite isolation with the assistance of reference laboratories.  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend attempting parasite isolation with the assistance of reference laboratories."
4. We recommend that clinicians contact their leishmaniasis reference laboratory before collecting specimens (Table 2).  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend that clinicians contact their leishmaniasis reference laboratory before collecting specimens (Table 2)."
5. We suggest that identification of the infecting parasite to the species level be attempted in cases of suspected CL.  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that identification of the infecting parasite to the species level be attempted in cases of suspected CL."
6. We suggest that tests for antileishmanial antibodies not be performed as the sole diagnostic assay.  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that tests for antileishmanial antibodies not be performed as the sole diagnostic assay."
7. We recommend that immunocompetent persons with skin lesions that are caused by infection with Leishmania species that are not associated with increased risk for ML, that are defined as clinically simple lesions (Table 1), and that are healing spontaneously may be observed without treatment if the patient concurs with this management [Strong, moderate].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend that immunocompetent persons with skin lesions that are caused by infection with Leishmania species that are not associated with increased risk for ML, that are defined as clinically simple lesions (Table 1), and that are healing spontaneously may be observed without treatment if the patient concurs with this management [Strong, moderate]."
8. We suggest that systemic treatment be offered for persons even with healing/recently healed CL lesions caused by increased ML-risk species or when the species is unknown but the infection was acquired in an increased ML-risk region.  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that systemic treatment be offered for persons even with healing/recently healed CL lesions caused by increased ML-risk species or when the species is unknown but the infection was acquired in an increased ML-risk region."
9. We recommend that any decision to observe a patient with CL without treatment should be reevaluated periodically, and the decision not to treat should be reconsidered if healing does not progress as anticipated [Strong, very low].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend that any decision to observe a patient with CL without treatment should be reevaluated periodically, and the decision not to treat should be reconsidered if healing does not progress as anticipated [Strong, very low]."
10. We recommend that therapeutic failure be assessed by physical appearance.  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend that therapeutic failure be assessed by physical appearance."
11. We recommend inpatient monitoring and prophylactic corticosteroid therapy for persons with laryngeal/pharyngeal disease and increased risk for respiratory obstruction, as indicated by symptoms and otolaryngologic/radiologic examinations, because of the potential for inflammatory reactions after initiation of antileishmanial therapy [Strong, low].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend inpatient monitoring and prophylactic corticosteroid therapy for persons with laryngeal/pharyngeal disease and increased risk for respiratory obstruction, as indicated by symptoms and otolaryngologic/radiologic examinations, because of the potential for inflammatory reactions after initiation of antileishmanial therapy [Strong, low]."
12. We recommend that persons with clinical abnormalities compatible with VL and laboratory evidence of VL be treated (Table 3) [Strong, moderate].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend that persons with clinical abnormalities compatible with VL and laboratory evidence of VL be treated (Table 3) [Strong, moderate]."
13. We suggest that clinicians closely monitor persons with asymptomatic visceral infection and generally initiate therapy only if clinical manifestations of VL develop [Weak, very low].  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that clinicians closely monitor persons with asymptomatic visceral infection and generally initiate therapy only if clinical manifestations of VL develop [Weak, very low]."
14. We recommend administering secondary prophylaxis (chronic maintenance therapy) to decrease the risk for posttreatment relapse of VL in persons with HIV/AIDS-associated immunosuppression (e.g., CD4 T-lymphocyte cell counts <200 cells/mm3) [Strong, moderate].  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend administering secondary prophylaxis (chronic maintenance therapy) to decrease the risk for posttreatment relapse of VL in persons with HIV/AIDS-associated immunosuppression (e.g., CD4 T-lymphocyte cell counts <200 cells/mm3) [Strong, moderate]."
15. We suggest that clinicians not routinely perform diagnostic testing to assess persons for evidence of asymptomatic visceral infection, including persons who have lived or traveled in leishmaniasis-endemic regions (Figure 3 ) and are considering organ transplantation or initiation of therapy with biologic immunomodulating agents.  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that clinicians not routinely perform diagnostic testing to assess persons for evidence of asymptomatic visceral infection, including persons who have lived or traveled in leishmaniasis-endemic regions (Figure 3 ) and are considering organ transplantation or initiation of therapy with biologic immunomodulating agents."
16. We suggest that CL/ML associated with the use of tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-alpha antagonist therapy be managed with systemic therapy and standard drug regimens for the pertinent setting/species (e.g., geographic area where the infection was acquired) [Weak, very low].  
   *Anchor:* "We suggest that CL/ML associated with the use of tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-alpha antagonist therapy be managed with systemic therapy and standard drug regimens for the pertinent setting/species (e.g., geographic area where the infection was acquired) [Weak, very low]."
