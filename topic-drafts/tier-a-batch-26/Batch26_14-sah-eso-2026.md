# Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage management: evidence record (batch 26, not published)

**Lead guideline:** European Stroke Organisation (ESO), European Association of Neurosurgical Societies (EANS) and European Society for Minimally Invasive Neurological Therapy (ESMINT) guideline on aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (2026), PMID 42095754, document id 6957. Read in full from the stored text.

## Synopsis (gist)

The spreadsheet lead is a review of neuroinflammation after aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage and is not a management guideline. This file uses the European Stroke Organisation, EANS and ESMINT guideline. The stored text recommends oral nimodipine and coiling rather than clipping when both are suitable in patients in good condition, says patients should not receive antifibrinolytic drugs to reduce rebleeding, that all patients should receive oral nimodipine but not statins, magnesium sulphate or endothelin-receptor antagonists, that blood pressure should be lowered slowly, that the ruptured aneurysm should ideally be treated within 24 hours by an experienced physician, and that decisions should be made by a multidisciplinary team. Volume and follow-up sentences are listed as stored.

## All recommendations of the lead guideline (18 statements + 0 body-text details)

### ESO/EANS/ESMINT recommendations

1. we recommend oral administration of nimodipine and regular coiling over clipping if both aneurysm treatment options are equally suitable in patients who are in good clinical condition, and recommend against the routine use of antifibrinolytic drugs prior to aneurysm treatment and against the use of tirilazad, statins, magnesium sulphate or endothelin receptor antagonists.  
   *Anchor:* "we recommend oral administration of nimodipine and regular coiling over clipping if both aneurysm treatment options are equally suitable in patients who are in good clinical condition, and recommend against the routine use of antifibrinolytic drugs prior to aneurysm treatment and against the use of tirilazad, statins, magnesium sulphate or endothelin receptor antagonists."
2. We recommend investigator-initiated RCTs in which the safety, efficacy and durability of new devices are compared with existing treatment options, with predefined outcomes that are assessed by independent outcome assessors without industry involvement.  
   *Anchor:* "We recommend investigator-initiated RCTs in which the safety, efficacy and durability of new devices are compared with existing treatment options, with predefined outcomes that are assessed by independent outcome assessors without industry involvement."
3. we suggest that the multidisciplinary team discusses the safety, effectiveness and durability of microsurgical and alternative endovascular options to determine the preferred treatment strategy, given the limited comparative evidence available.  
   *Anchor:* "we suggest that the multidisciplinary team discusses the safety, effectiveness and durability of microsurgical and alternative endovascular options to determine the preferred treatment strategy, given the limited comparative evidence available."
4. we suggest that this treatment is only performed if other treatment options are not feasible.  
   *Anchor:* "we suggest that this treatment is only performed if other treatment options are not feasible."
5. we suggest to reserve flow diversion as a last-line treatment option.  
   *Anchor:* "we suggest to reserve flow diversion as a last-line treatment option."
6. Based on a high level of scientific evidence, this guideline recommends that patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage should not receive antifibrinolytic drugs to reduce the risk of rebleeding, as they do not decrease the risk of poor outcome.  
   *Anchor:* "Based on a high level of scientific evidence, this guideline recommends that patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage should not receive antifibrinolytic drugs to reduce the risk of rebleeding, as they do not decrease the risk of poor outcome."
7. All patients should receive oral nimodipine, a drug that reduces the risk of brain damage, but not other drugs such as statins, magnesium sulphate or endothelin receptor antagonists.  
   *Anchor:* "All patients should receive oral nimodipine, a drug that reduces the risk of brain damage, but not other drugs such as statins, magnesium sulphate or endothelin receptor antagonists."
8. Blood pressure lowering should be performed slowly and in a controlled manner, ie, without undershoot, and with short-acting medications.  
   *Anchor:* "Blood pressure lowering should be performed slowly and in a controlled manner, ie, without undershoot, and with short-acting medications."
9. The ruptured aneurysm should ideally be treated within 24 h, when possible, and by an experienced physician who treats at least 30 intracranial aneurysms (either ruptured or unruptured) per year.  
   *Anchor:* "The ruptured aneurysm should ideally be treated within 24 h, when possible, and by an experienced physician who treats at least 30 intracranial aneurysms (either ruptured or unruptured) per year."
10. Treatment decisions should always be made by a multidisciplinary team (composed of at least 1 neurosurgeon and 1 neurointerventionalist) to consider the different aneurysm treatment options, and decide on the safest method, and the most effective and durable treatment outcome.  
   *Anchor:* "Treatment decisions should always be made by a multidisciplinary team (composed of at least 1 neurosurgeon and 1 neurointerventionalist) to consider the different aneurysm treatment options, and decide on the safest method, and the most effective and durable treatment outcome."
11. Endovascular drug treatments may be used in patients with a critically reduced blood flow to the brain due to severe narrowing of the brain arteries, but treatments that widen the arteries by the use of inflatable balloons should only be considered as a last-line rescue treatment after careful multidisciplinary discussion of the potential risks and uncertain benefits.  
   *Anchor:* "Endovascular drug treatments may be used in patients with a critically reduced blood flow to the brain due to severe narrowing of the brain arteries, but treatments that widen the arteries by the use of inflatable balloons should only be considered as a last-line rescue treatment after careful multidisciplinary discussion of the potential risks and uncertain benefits."
12. Long-term fluid drainage devices should be used in patients with chronic hydrocephalus when temporary drainage is no longer safe.  
   *Anchor:* "Long-term fluid drainage devices should be used in patients with chronic hydrocephalus when temporary drainage is no longer safe."
13. Patients should be treated in a specialised neuro-ICU in centres that treat at least 70 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage per year, or at least 35 in remote areas.  
   *Anchor:* "Patients should be treated in a specialised neuro-ICU in centres that treat at least 70 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage per year, or at least 35 in remote areas."
14. Follow-up imaging is only indicated as long as re-treatment of the aneurysm remains an option, and in the following situations that depend on type of aneurysm treatment: Patients treated with coils should have follow-up imaging at least once at or after 12 months after aneurysm treatment.  
   *Anchor:* "Follow-up imaging is only indicated as long as re-treatment of the aneurysm remains an option, and in the following situations that depend on type of aneurysm treatment: Patients treated with coils should have follow-up imaging at least once at or after 12 months after aneurysm treatment."
15. Patients who are at high risk of developing new aneurysms, such as those with a young age, positive family history for aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage or autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, should have scans every 5 years to check for new aneurysms.  
   *Anchor:* "Patients who are at high risk of developing new aneurysms, such as those with a young age, positive family history for aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage or autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, should have scans every 5 years to check for new aneurysms."

The 2013 guideline suggested treating the ruptured aneurysm “as early as logistically and technically possible to reduce the risk of rebleeding; if possible, it should be aimed to intervene at least within 72 h after onset of first symptoms.” This was based on a small randomised trial,34 which showed a trend towards better outcomes in patients operated within 3 days after the bleeding.
