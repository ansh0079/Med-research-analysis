## 851 — Huntington disease chorea management

topicId: `d533e98d-da7d-420a-8d00-f3440764ad9a`

### Mentor intro
Huntington disease has no disease-modifying therapy, so chorea management sits at the centre of what clinicians can actually offer. Chorea â€” random, jerk-like movements migrating between body parts â€” is the most common motor manifestation, interfering with daily activities, injuring patients and eroding quality of life. The therapeutic mainstay is VMAT2 inhibition: tetrabenazine's legacy is real efficacy weighed against depression and parkinsonism, while deutetrabenazine's deuterium substitution confers a longer half-life, less-frequent dosing and an improved tolerability profile â€” the reason it became the modern default.

### Teaching points
- Chorea is defined as jerk-like movements moving randomly between body parts, caused by various disorders; current symptomatic therapy is quite effective, though few aetiology-targeted therapies exist. [[PMID 29120264]]
- Deutetrabenazine is FDA-approved for chorea in Huntington's disease; deuterium substitution gives a longer half-life allowing less-frequent daily dosing, and as a reversible VMAT2 inhibitor it depletes monoamines. [[PMID 30624137]]
- In Huntington's disease, an inherited neurodegenerative disorder with no disease-modifying treatment, chorea is the most common motor manifestation and may interfere with daily activities, reduce quality of life and cause injury. [[PMID 29996061]]

### Uncertainties
- Whether suppressing chorea with VMAT2 inhibitors alters long-term functional outcomes beyond symptom relief is unknown.
- Treatment of the non-choreic motor and neuropsychiatric burden (bradykinesia, depression, suicidality) needs separate, individualised strategies.

## 852 — hybrid closed loop insulin delivery in type 1 diabetes

topicId: `37c98f5d-2c5e-4539-aebf-6072e551cacc`

### Mentor intro
The hybrid closed loop system is the closest thing to an artificial pancreas that mainstream care currently has: a continuous glucose sensor feeding an algorithm that adjusts pump-delivered insulin in real time, with the user still announcing meals. Building on pump and CGM technology that already improves HbA1c, time-in-range and hypoglycaemia burden, closed loops reduce the daily cognitive load of type 1 diabetes. The field has now matured enough for network meta-analysis to compare competing systems head-to-head, which is exactly the evidence clinicians need when choosing between them.

### Teaching points
- Diabetes technologies represent a paradigm shift in type 1 diabetes care: pumps and continuous glucose monitors improve HbA1c, enhance time in range, limit severe hypoglycaemia and reduce diabetes distress, and the artificial pancreas extends this trajectory. [[PMID 36734145]]
- A network meta-analysis of randomised trials compares the efficacy and safety of different hybrid closed loop systems, including the Minimed 670G class, across children, adolescents and adults. [[PMID 39298688]]

### Uncertainties
- Long-term durability of glycaemic gains and real-world adherence outside trial conditions are less certain.
- Which system performs best for which patient phenotype (hypoglycaemia-prone, adolescents, highly variable schedules) is being refined.

### MCQ angles

- Glycaemic outcomes: time-in-range, HbA1c, hypoglycaemia reduction.
