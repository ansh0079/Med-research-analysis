## 850 — HRS diagnosis

topicId: `3f7e1be4-bf93-4b83-9d80-a9076714c54a`

### Mentor intro
Hepatorenal syndrome is the extreme manifestation of kidney dysfunction in cirrhosis â€” renal hypoperfusion driven by splanchnic vasodilation and effective arterial underfilling, not by intrinsic renal disease. Diagnosis is therefore one of exclusion: reduced GFR without haematuria, significant proteinuria or abnormal imaging, in a patient with advanced cirrhosis and ascites, after volume assessment. Modern nomenclature (HRS-AKI replacing the old type 1/2 dichotomy) ties it to the AKI framework, and treatment rests on vasoconstrictors with albumin â€” with terlipressin now backed by randomised-trial evidence.

### Teaching points
- HRS is diagnosed when kidney function is reduced without evidence of intrinsic kidney disease such as haematuria, proteinuria or abnormal kidney imaging; it reflects reduced renal blood flow and GFR as the extreme of circulatory dysfunction in cirrhosis. [[PMID 32928750]]
- HRS characteristically occurs in advanced cirrhosis, driven by circulatory and haemodynamic alterations aggravated by systemic inflammation and bacterial translocation. [[PMID 38246506]]
- An ADQI/International Club of Ascites consensus addresses AKI in cirrhosis, a complication markedly increasing in-hospital morbidity and mortality, with HRS a specific phenotype. [[PMID 38527522]]
- Cirrhosis progresses through compensated, decompensated and further-decompensated stages with median survival of more than 15 years, 2 years and 9 months respectively, with worsening portal hypertension and vasodilatory dysfunction driving complications such as HRS. [[PMID 37978969]]

### Uncertainties
- Diagnostic cut-offs (creatinine thresholds in sarcopenic cirrhotics) are imperfect, and biomarkers distinguishing HRS from ATN remain investigational.
- Optimal vasoconstrictor selection (terlipressin versus noradrenaline) and albumin dosing schedules vary by setting.

### MCQ angles
- HRS diagnostic criteria and the exclusion of intrinsic renal disease.
- Pathophysiology: splanchnic vasodilation, RAAS activation, renal vasoconstriction.
- HRS-AKI versus HRS-CKD under current nomenclature.
- Treatment: terlipressin plus albumin, and liver transplantation as definitive therapy.

### Case hooks
- A cirrhotic patient with ascites has creatinine rising over 72 hours after diuretics; distinguish HRS-AKI from prerenal azotaemia and ATN.
