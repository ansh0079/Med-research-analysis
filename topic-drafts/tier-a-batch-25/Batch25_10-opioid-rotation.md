# Opioid Rotation and Equianalgesic Dosing: evidence record (batch 25, not published)

**Lead guideline:** Practical management of opioid rotation and equianalgesia (2018), PMID 30464578, document id 7741. Read in full from the stored text.

## Synopsis (gist)

The stored text is a 2018 review of opioid rotation and equianalgesia in cancer pain. Sentences that state a conversion ratio, a directional difference, or a practical step during rotation are listed. The equianalgesia table is not reconstructed where it does not appear as a sentence. The CDC opioid prescribing guidelines were not used.

## All recommendations of the lead guideline (9 statements + 0 body-text details)

### Opioid rotation review

1. Despite the daily use of equianalgesic tables, they are not based on high-level scientific evidence.  
   *Anchor:* "despite the daily use of equianalgesic tables, they are not based on high-level scientific evidence."
2. Opioid rotation is also particularly useful if a patient becomes tolerant to a given opioid drug, develops hyperalgesia  
   *Anchor:* "opioid rotation is also particularly useful if a patient becomes tolerant to a given opioid drug, develops hyperalgesia"
3. Rotation may also be indicated by a change in the patient's clinical state, such as kidney failure, liver failure, fever, and other disorders that may impair the pharmacokinetics or metabolism of opioid drugs.  
   *Anchor:* "rotation may also be indicated by a change in the patient's clinical state, such as kidney failure, liver failure, fever, and other disorders that may impair the pharmacokinetics or metabolism of opioid drugs."
4. These studies demonstrate considerable variability in conversion ratios, both within and across studies, and underscore the need for caution in applying ratios during opioid rotation from and to fentanyl.  
   *Anchor:* "these studies demonstrate considerable variability in conversion ratios, both within and across studies, and underscore the need for caution in applying ratios during opioid rotation from and to fentanyl."
5. A calculation table with a ratio 100:1 is by 30% subequianalgesic but may be appropriate with allowance of a safety margin.  
   *Anchor:* "a calculation table with a ratio 100:1 is by 30% subequianalgesic but may be appropriate with allowance of a safety margin."
6. Patients receiving oral morphine before oxycodone therapy should have their daily dose based on the following ratio: 10 mg of oral oxycodone is equivalent to 20 mg of oral morphine.  
   *Anchor:* "patients receiving oral morphine before oxycodone therapy should have their daily dose based on the following ratio: 10 mg of oral oxycodone is equivalent to 20 mg of oral morphine."
7. The authors therefore recommended a ratio of 5 for m:hm in rotating from m to hm and ratio of 3.7 for m:hm when rotating from hm to m in patients exposed to chronic intake of these opioids.  
   *Anchor:* "the authors therefore recommended a ratio of 5 for m:hm in rotating from m to hm and ratio of 3.7 for m:hm when rotating from hm to m in patients exposed to chronic intake of these opioids."
8. Webster and fine recommend providing sufficient immediate-release opioid throughout the rotation to prevent withdrawal and/or increased pain if the dosing changes prove insufficient.  
   *Anchor:* "webster and fine recommend providing sufficient immediate-release opioid throughout the rotation to prevent withdrawal and/or increased pain if the dosing changes prove insufficient."
9. Although most experts recommend the use of equianalgesia tables (due to their ease of use, clear clinical value, and good long-term safety record), they also acknowledge that currently available tools are not ideal.  
   *Anchor:* "although most experts recommend the use of equianalgesia tables (due to their ease of use, clear clinical value, and good long-term safety record), they also acknowledge that currently available tools are not ideal."
