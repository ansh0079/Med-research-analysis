# Acute phosphate nephropathy: evidence record (batch 27, not published)

**Lead guideline:** Acute Phosphate Nephropathy Following Oral Sodium Phosphate Bowel Preparation. (year not stored), PMID 42553916, document id 9279. Read in full from the stored text.

## Synopsis (gist)

The stored text states that acute phosphate nephropathy is a rare cause of acute kidney injury, most commonly after oral sodium phosphate bowel preparation before colonoscopy. It lists risk factors that include chronic kidney disease, older age, female sex, dehydration, diabetes, hypertension, and nephrotoxic drugs. It says phosphate-containing bowel preparations should be avoided when those predisposing factors are present. Management is described as supportive: stop nephrotoxic agents, give intravenous hydration, correct electrolytes, and use renal replacement therapy when clinically indicated. Early recognition, with withdrawal of the offending agent, is described as important for renal recovery.

### Statements in the stored text

1. Acute phosphate nephropathy (APN) is a rare but important cause of acute kidney injury (AKI), most commonly associated with the use of oral sodium phosphate bowel preparations administered prior to colonoscopy.  
   *Anchor:* "Acute phosphate nephropathy (APN) is a rare but important cause of acute kidney injury (AKI), most commonly associated with the use of oral sodium phosphate bowel preparations administered prior to colonoscopy."
2. Risk factors include chronic kidney disease, advanced age, female sex, dehydration, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and concurrent use of nephrotoxic medications such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors.  
   *Anchor:* "Risk factors include chronic kidney disease, advanced age, female sex, dehydration, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and concurrent use of nephrotoxic medications such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors"
3. Early recognition is important because prompt withdrawal of the offending agent and supportive management may improve renal recovery and help prevent irreversible kidney damage.  
   *Anchor:* "Early recognition is important because prompt withdrawal of the offending agent and supportive management may improve renal recovery and help prevent irreversible kidney damage"
4. Phosphate-containing bowel preparations should be avoided in patients with predisposing factors, including diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dehydration, chronic kidney disease, or concurrent use of nephrotoxic medications.  
   *Anchor:* "Phosphate-containing bowel preparations should be avoided in patients with predisposing factors, including diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dehydration, chronic kidney disease, or concurrent use of nephrotoxic medications"
5. Management of APN is primarily supportive and includes discontinuation of nephrotoxic agents, aggressive intravenous hydration, correction of electrolyte abnormalities, and renal replacement therapy when clinically indicated.  
   *Anchor:* "Management of APN is primarily supportive and includes discontinuation of nephrotoxic agents, aggressive intravenous hydration, correction of electrolyte abnormalities, and renal replacement therapy when clinically indicated."
