## #771 healthcare associated mrsa decolonization and prevention  (cluster `MRSA decolonization and prevention`)

**Mentor intro.** S. aureus is a leading cause of healthcare-associated infection â€” central-line bloodstream infections, surgical site infections, and more â€” and nasal carriage is the reservoir most interventions target. The trainee should understand who to screen, what regimens exist, and where the evidence is strongest â€” cardiac and orthopaedic surgery â€” versus where it is weaker.

**Teaching points**
- S. aureus is a common skin commensal and a leading cause of healthcare-associated infections including central-line-associated bloodstream infection and surgical site infection, which makes decolonization a central prevention strategy. [[PMID 42624765]]
- Nasal colonization with MRSA is the key target for eradication interventions, and multiple interventions targeting the nasal microbiome have been developed for this purpose. [[PMID 39481681]]
- Healthcare-adapted MRSA spread globally over 50 years, and community strains capable of infecting healthy individuals have since emerged; control requires strategies for both healthcare- and community-associated spread. [[PMID 29190327]]
- S. aureus is the most common microbial cause of surgical site infections, and screening with decolonization before surgery is used to reduce SSI risk, particularly in high-risk procedures. [[PMID 27424948]]
- Staphylococcal screening and suppression is an interventional strategy aimed at reduction of surgical site infections; its effectiveness depends on the screening strategy and agent used. [[PMID 26836053]]

**Uncertainties**
- Optimal decolonization regimen, duration, and the role of systemic versus topical agents are not settled in the retained abstracts.

**MCQ angles**

**Case hooks**

## #772 heart failure  (cluster `heart failure`)

**Mentor intro.** Heart failure is a clinical syndrome â€” dyspnoea or exertional limitation from impaired ventricular filling or ejection â€” that now divides by ejection fraction, because therapy that transforms HFrEF does little or nothing for HFpEF beyond SGLT2 inhibitors. Anchor on the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline as the framework, then learn the phenotype split (roughly half of all HF is preserved EF) and the four pillars of HFrEF disease-modifying therapy.

**Teaching points**
- The 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline replaces both the 2013 ACCF/AHA guideline and the 2017 focused update, and is the current American framework for heart failure management. [[PMID 35363499]]
- The 2023 ESC focused update introduced key new recommendations led by SGLT2 inhibitors across the ejection-fraction spectrum, reflecting major recent progress in HF management. [[PMID 38806171]]
- Worldwide HF burden has grown to an estimated 23 million people, with approximately half of cases being HFrEF; HF is a clinical syndrome of dyspnoea or exertional limitation from impaired ventricular filling or ejection. [[PMID 32749493]]
- HFpEF represents at least half of all heart failure cases, and its recognition is rising with population ageing and comorbidity burden such as obesity and diabetes. [[PMID 38367642]]
- HF prevalence is increasing because of population ageing and improved survival after acute cardiovascular events, despite effective therapies â€” making long-term management a growing clinical load. [[PMID 28460827]]

**Uncertainties**
- HFpEF treatment beyond SGLT2 inhibitors remains an active research area with no established disease-modifying standard comparable to HFrEF quadruple therapy.
- Optimal transitions of care and the role of remote monitoring in routine HF management are not settled in the retained sources.

**MCQ angles**

- The four pillars of HFrEF therapy and the guideline evidence behind each.
- SGLT2 inhibitors as the one class spanning all EF categories.
- Distinguishing HFpEF clinically (comorbidity pattern, diastolic dysfunction) from HFrEF.

**Case hooks**

## #773 Heart failure landmark trials: PARADIGM-HF, DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced insights  (cluster `heart failure landmark trials`)

**Mentor intro.** Three trials reset HFrEF practice. PARADIGM-HF showed sacubitril/valsartan beat enalapril; DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced showed SGLT2 inhibitors cut cardiovascular death and heart-failure hospitalisation in patients with and without diabetes. Together they form the spine of modern guideline-directed therapy â€” and this page reads them as a set, including the sobering finding that real-world patients often fail the trials' eligibility criteria.

**Teaching points**
- Landmark trials established new treatments for HFrEF: sacubitril/valsartan was superior to enalapril in PARADIGM-HF, and SGLT2 inhibitors were added to the therapeutic armamentarium. [[PMID 33277825]]
- A network meta-analysis compared the treatment arms of sacubitril/valsartan, vericiguat and the SGLT2 inhibitors dapagliflozin and empagliflozin, each proven effective in phase 3 HFrEF trials, allowing relative efficacy to be estimated across drugs. [[PMID 33074526]]
- When eligibility criteria from DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced and PARADIGM-HF are applied to real outpatient populations with chronic heart failure, a substantial proportion of routine patients fall outside trial inclusion criteria â€” a key caveat when extrapolating trial effect sizes to clinic practice. [[PMID 37952788]]
- Cross-trial aggregate-data analyses estimate the expected combined benefit of comprehensive (quintuple) disease-modifying pharmacological treatment in HFrEF, suggesting effects accumulate when therapies are combined. [[PMID 42741561]]

**Uncertainties**
- Cross-trial and network meta-analyses carry indirect-comparison limitations; true head-to-head outcome data between individual agents are lacking.
- How trial-ineligible patients (eGFR limits, blood-pressure floors, recent hospitalisation) respond to the same drugs is less certain.

**MCQ angles**
- What PARADIGM-HF compared and what the primary outcome showed (ARNI vs ACE inhibitor).
- DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced: the populations, primary endpoints, and diabetes-independence of SGLT2 benefit.
- Why real-world eligibility for landmark trials matters when applying effect sizes.
- How relative efficacy estimates from network meta-analyses should (and should not) be read.

**Case hooks**
- A 68-year-old with HFrEF, eGFR 42 and SBP 98 asks whether the 'four pillars' apply to him; use trial eligibility thinking.
- An exam question quotes PARADIGM-HF, DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced hazard ratios; rank the therapies and justify combination.

**Gaps for the reviewer.** Exact hazard ratios and subgroup findings for each trial are not in the retained abstracts; verify numerical details against the primary trial publications before writing numeric MCQs.

**Sources**
- PMID 33277825: Heart failure in the last year: progress and perspective. (review, 2020)
- PMID 33074526: Relative Efficacy of Sacubitril-Valsartan, Vericiguat, and SGLT2 Inhibitors in Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction: A Network Meta-Analysis. (meta-analysis, 2021)
- PMID 37952788: Application of eligibility criteria from DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, and PARADIGM-HF trials to a population with heart failure. (review, 2024)

## #774 heart failure preserved ejection fraction SGLT2 inhibitor mortality  (cluster `HFpEF SGLT2 inhibitors`)

**Mentor intro.** For years HFpEF had no therapy that moved outcomes â€” every trial failed until SGLT2 inhibitors. EMPEROR-Preserved showed empagliflozin reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation in HFpEF, driven by fewer worsening-HF events, positioning SGLT2 inhibitors as the new standard of care in this phenotype. **Teaching points**
- In EMPEROR-Preserved, empagliflozin reduced the risk of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure in HFpEF (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69-0.90, P<.001), an effect driven by a reduction in worsening HF events; SGLT2 inhibitors are therefore likely to become the new standard of care in HFpEF. ## #776 heart failure reduced ejection fraction treatment  (cluster `HFrEF treatment`)

**Mentor intro.** HFrEF is the phenotype where drugs reliably save lives. Network meta-analyses of the contemporary trials converge on the same message: the combination of beta-blocker, ARNI, MRA and SGLT2 inhibitor is the most effective pharmacological strategy for reducing mortality. The clinical gap is implementation â€” most eligible patients never reach target doses of all four classes. Learn the evidence hierarchy, then learn how to actually get the drugs in.

**Teaching points**
- The 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline is the current American management framework for heart failure including HFrEF, replacing the 2013 guideline and 2017 focused update. [[PMID 35363499]]
- A 2022 network meta-analysis estimated and compared aggregate treatment benefits of pharmacological therapy combinations in HFrEF, because the effects of contemporary therapy combinations were not well characterised. [[PMID 34895860]]
- The 2025 updated network meta-analysis found that a combination of beta-blockers, ARNi, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors was the most effective pharmacological approach for reducing all-cause mortality. [[PMID 40892608]]
- HFrEF makes up approximately half of the worldwide heart-failure burden (an estimated 23 million people), and presents as dyspnoea or exertional limitation from impaired ventricular ejection. [[PMID 32749493]]
- SGLT2 inhibitors have been studied across the cardiometabolic spectrum (heart failure, type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, post-myocardial infarction), with heart-failure outcome benefits that extend beyond any single disease context. [[PMID 38768620]]

**Uncertainties**
- How quickly each component can be uptitrated in hypotensive or renal-impaired patients, and the safety of simultaneous initiation, is not quantified in the retained abstracts.
- Whether newer agents (vericiguat, omecamtiv) add to quadruple therapy in routine practice is not resolved in the retained sources.

**MCQ angles**

- Why combination therapy beats any single agent escalation in HFrEF.
- SGLT2 inhibitor benefits across cardiometabolic comorbidity contexts.
- Clinical inertia: why eligible patients miss target doses and how to structure titration.

**Case hooks**

**Gaps for the reviewer.** Device therapy (ICD/CRT) indications, acute decompensated HFrEF management, and specific dosing/titration tables are not covered by the retained abstracts; verify against the full guideline before approval.

## #777 Heart failure remote hemodynamic monitoring  (cluster `heart failure remote monitoring`)

Remote haemodynamic monitoring â€” led by the pulmonary-artery pressure sensor (CardioMEMS) â€” aims to catch that rise and act on it before the patient decompensates. The evidence landscape is mixed, however: devices are approved and clinically available, but questions about which patients benefit and how programmes should be implemented remain live.

**Teaching points**
- Wireless haemodynamic monitoring in heart failure allows volume assessment without physical examination, and data from these devices are used to assist patient management and avoid heart-failure hospitalisations. [[PMID 33420917]]
- Interest in remote haemodynamic monitoring is driven by persistently high HF mortality despite treatment advances, and by the rising global prevalence of HF and its burden on hospital resources. [[PMID 38109949]]
- Traditional management based on intermittent clinic visits and subjective symptom reporting often fails to detect early decompensation, motivating a monitoring landscape that includes both implantable and non-implantable technologies. [[PMID 41233030]]
- Several devices are available for outpatient heart-failure management, but implementation requires practical clinical recommendations for integrating them into daily practice. [[PMID 39131070]] [[PMID 38567948]]
- The CardioMEMS pulmonary artery pressure sensor is FDA approved and CE marked for remote haemodynamic monitoring to reduce HF hospitalisations, though the clinical evidence base continues to be appraised. [[PMID 37070597]]

**Uncertainties**
- Which HF subpopulation (recently hospitalised vs stable chronic) gains most from PA-pressure monitoring is not settled in the retained abstracts.
- Health-economic value and reimbursement vary by system; cost-effectiveness is not resolved in the retained sources.

**MCQ angles**

**Case hooks**

## #778 Heart failure remote monitoring: biomarkers, implantable sensors, telemonitoring  (cluster `heart failure remote monitoring`)

**Mentor intro.** Remote HF monitoring has three lanes: blood biomarkers (BNP/NT-proBNP), device-based sensors already present in many patients (pacemakers and defibrillators that track impedance and other trends), and structured telemonitoring. Each lane detects different signals at different time horizons, and each has distinct failure modes â€” biomarkers fluctuate with renal function, device algorithms alarm imperfectly, and telemonitoring trials have been mixed. The skill is matching the lane to the patient.

**Teaching points**
- B-type natriuretic peptide, recognised since 1988 as a powerful cardiovascular biomarker, is central to heart-failure monitoring, with ongoing effort devoted to precise assay and biosensor-based measurement. [[PMID 26979601]]
- Cardiac implantable electronic devices include remote monitoring tools intended to predict worsening heart failure and avert hospitalisations, but device measurements such as intrathoracic impedance face practical and interpretive challenges. [[PMID 26663507]]
- HF symptoms reflect elevated ventricular filling pressures, and tests that characterise diastolic function help identify the pressure elevation that precedes decompensation â€” informing how often and how to test. [[PMID 31202743]]
- Digital biomarkers derived from digital health technologies enable quantifiable, near-continuous, real-world capture of pathophysiological signals, and their role in cardiovascular medicine is formalised in an ESC Working Group scientific statement. [[PMID 42491935]]

**Uncertainties**
- Whether routine natriuretic-peptide-guided titration improves outcomes in routine practice (beyond trial settings) is not settled in the retained abstracts.
- Which combinations of biomarker, device and telemonitoring signals add predictive value over any single lane is unresolved.

**MCQ angles**
- BNP/NT-proBNP: what raises it besides HF (renal dysfunction, age, AF) and how that limits guided therapy.
- Device-measured intrathoracic impedance: promise and false-alarm limitations.
- Diastolic-function testing and filling-pressure estimation in monitoring.
- Digital biomarkers as a formal monitoring category (ESC framing).

**Case hooks**
- A patient's BNP doubles but creatinine has also risen; decide whether this is decompensation, renal deterioration, or both.
- A CRT device flags rising impedance trends in an asymptomatic patient; plan the response.

**Gaps for the reviewer.** Telemonitoring RCT evidence (e.g., TIM-HF2) and weight/blood-pressure home-monitoring trial data are not in the retained abstracts; verify before approval.

**Sources**

## #779 Heart failure SGLT2 inhibitor treatment  (cluster `SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure`)

**Mentor intro.** SGLT2 inhibitors are the defining heart-failure drug story of this decade: a diabetes drug class that turned out to reduce cardiovascular death and HF hospitalisation in HFrEF patients with and without diabetes, then extended benefit into HFpEF. **Teaching points**

- In the DAPA ACT HF-TIMI 68 trial, in-hospital initiation of dapagliflozin did not significantly reduce cardiovascular death or worsening HF through 2 months in hospitalised HF patients; however, the totality of randomised trial data suggests in-hospital SGLT2i initiation may reduce early cardiovascular death or worsening HF and all-cause mortality. [[PMID 40884036]]
- The 2023 ESC focused update made SGLT2 inhibitors a headline recommendation in HF management, reflecting trial results that now span the ejection-fraction spectrum. [[PMID 38806171]]
- SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists entered cardiology from glucose-lowering therapy, with cardiovascular outcome trial evidence establishing protection beyond glycaemic control â€” including in heart failure. [[PMID 34216571]]
- Collaborative meta-analysis of large placebo-controlled trials confirmed that SGLT2 inhibitors reduce adverse kidney and cardiovascular outcomes across patients with heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or type 2 diabetes with high atherosclerotic risk, informing how diabetes status modulates benefit. [[PMID 36351458]]

**Uncertainties**
- The signal for benefit of starting SGLT2 inhibitors during an acute HF admission is suggestive from pooled data but not proven by the individual DAPA ACT HF-TIMI 68 trial.
- Optimal duration and whether effects persist through advanced CKD (stage 4) are incompletely defined in the retained sources.

**MCQ angles**
- DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced: populations and the diabetes-independence of benefit.
- Interpreting a neutral individual trial (DAPA ACT HF-TIMI 68) alongside a supportive meta-analysis.
- Mechanistic shift: from glucose-lowering to cardiorenal protection drug class.
- Practical initiation: who, when, and what renal thresholds allow.

**Case hooks**
- A hospitalised HF patient is stable and diuresing on day 2; decide whether to start dapagliflozin before discharge and what to tell the team about the evidence.
- A non-diabetic patient asks why a 'diabetes drug' is being prescribed for his heart failure; explain the trial evidence.

**Gaps for the reviewer.** EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER HFpEF results and SGLT2i safety counselling (euglycaemic DKA, genital infections) are not detailed in the retained abstracts; verify before approval.

## #780 heart failure with preserved ejection fraction  (cluster `HFpEF`)

**Mentor intro.** HFpEF is at least half of all heart failure and the half with the fewest proven therapies. Diagnosis is the first hurdle â€” symptoms and signs are nonspecific and echo evidence of elevated filling pressures is required â€” and comorbidity management (hypertension, obesity, diabetes, atrial fibrillation) does most of the therapeutic work. SGLT2 inhibitors are now the one class with a positive HF-outcome trial in this phenotype, so the modern HFpEF consultation is diagnosis, phenotype, comorbidity control, and a dapagliflozin or empagliflozin prescription.

**Teaching points**
- HFpEF represents at least half of all heart-failure cases, driven by an ageing population and rising comorbidities such as obesity and diabetes; it is increasingly recognised in clinical practice. [[PMID 38367642]]
- HFpEF accounts for nearly half of all heart-failure cases with rising prevalence, is associated with significant morbidity and mortality, and carries specific risk factors that should structure work-up. [[PMID 39143132]]
