**Topics this record serves:**
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage ICU management

**Scope:** ICU-facing statements in the 2023 AHA/ASA aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage guideline: seizures, nimodipine, blood pressure before the aneurysm is secured, devices in the acute phase, delayed cerebral ischaemia monitoring, and avoidance of prophylactic haemodynamic augmentation. The full guideline also covers diagnosis and aneurysm repair; those sections are not listed here.

## Synopsis

The stored guideline recommends 7 days of antiseizure medication for new seizures after aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage, and it says prophylactic antiseizure medication should not be routine. Nimodipine is described as beneficial for delayed cerebral ischaemia and functional outcome, and routine statin therapy and intravenous magnesium are not recommended. Before the aneurysm is secured, frequent blood-pressure monitoring with short-acting drugs is recommended. Stents and flow diverters are avoided in the acute phase when the rupture site can be secured by coiling or clipping. Frequent neurological checks with the GCS or NIHSS are recommended, the stored deterioration wording used here should last at least an hour and not be apparent immediately after aneurysm occlusion or attributable to other causes, and prophylactic haemodynamic augmentation and hypervolaemia are not performed.
