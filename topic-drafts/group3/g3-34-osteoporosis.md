## Synopsis

The stored recommendations say a FRAX assessment should be performed in any postmenopausal woman, or man aged 50 years or older, who has a clinical risk factor for fragility fracture. Clinical judgement is required when risk goes beyond the factors FRAX can enter, and arithmetic adjustments may account for extra factors such as falls. Femoral-neck T-scores use young healthy women from NHANES III as the reference. DXA is the stated reference technology at the femoral neck, results should be reported within 3 weeks by trained professionals, and quantitative ultrasound is not recommended for diagnosis. People with osteoporosis or a fragility fracture should be investigated for underlying causes.
