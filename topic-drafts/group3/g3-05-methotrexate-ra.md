**Scope:** The 2009 3E recommendations on methotrexate, focused on rheumatoid arthritis. Document 1856, the 2021 ACR rheumatoid arthritis guideline, was read and does not state a folate dose, so it was not the source for this topic.

## Synopsis

The stored 3E text says the work-up before methotrexate includes alcohol intake, education, AST, ALT, albumin, blood count, creatinine and a chest radiograph within the previous year. Oral methotrexate starts at 10–15 mg per week, rising by 5 mg every 2–4 weeks up to 20–30 mg per week, with parenteral treatment if response or tolerance is inadequate. At least 5 mg of folic acid per week is strongly recommended. ALT with or without AST, creatinine and the blood count are checked every 1–1.5 months until the dose is stable, then every 1–3 months. Methotrexate is stopped if a confirmed ALT or AST rise is greater than three times the upper limit of normal. It is described as appropriate for long-term use, may be continued through elective orthopaedic surgery in rheumatoid arthritis, and should not be used for at least 3 months before a planned pregnancy or during pregnancy or breastfeeding.
