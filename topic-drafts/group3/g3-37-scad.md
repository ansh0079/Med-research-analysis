## Synopsis

The stored statement says that once SCAD is suspected, coronary angiography should be performed as early as feasible, especially with ST-segment elevation. Intracoronary imaging is pursued only when the angiogram is uncertain and the vessel is large enough, and CT coronary angiography is not recommended as the first test in high-risk acute coronary syndrome. Urgent PCI or bypass is considered when needed, but the decision is individualised, and chest pain alone is not an indication for emergency revascularisation if there is no ischaemia or flow is normal. ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers are used when infarction has left ventricular systolic dysfunction. Patients are generally advised to avoid prolonged high-intensity activity, and repeat angiography is limited to situations where the benefit may outweigh the risk of iatrogenic dissection.
