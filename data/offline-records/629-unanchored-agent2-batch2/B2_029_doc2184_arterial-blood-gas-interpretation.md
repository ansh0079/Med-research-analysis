## Synopsis (gist)

Accurate arterial blood gas interpretation is essential for primary care providers, especially in emergency and inpatient settings where timely, informed decisions can significantly impact patient outcomes. Three primary methods exist for acid-base evaluation: the traditional (Boston), base excess (Copenhagen) and physicochemical (Stewart) methods, and all three methods generally yield correct clinical interpretation when used properly. Accurate interpretation is crucial to distinguish between simple acid-base disorders, where a primary disturbance and its compensation are present, and mixed disorders, where multiple primary disturbances occur simultaneously. Proper specimen collection and handling are vital, and samples should be placed on ice if immediate analysis is not possible to prevent oxygen metabolism by platelets and leukocytes. Venous blood gas analysis offers valuable pH and bicarbonate data when arterial access is challenging, but arterial blood gas remains essential for accurately evaluating oxygenation and ventilation, particularly in suspected or confirmed respiratory disorders or hypoxaemia. The stated recommendations are to obtain arterial and/or venous blood gas measurements when clinically indicated, interpret them confidently, and adhere to a structured, stepwise approach.
