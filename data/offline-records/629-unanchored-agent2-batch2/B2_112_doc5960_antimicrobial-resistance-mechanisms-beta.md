## Synopsis (gist)

In Enterobacteriaceae, resistance to third-generation cephalosporins is described as resulting mainly from plasmid-borne extended-spectrum beta-lactamases, especially the CTX-M family. The efficacy of beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor associations for severe infections due to ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae has not been adequately evaluated in critically ill patients, and carbapenems still stands as the first-line choice in this situation. In non-fermenting Gram-negative bacilli, multidrug resistance may follow sequential chromosomal mutations leading to overproduction of intrinsic beta-lactamases, hyper-expression of efflux pumps, target modifications, and permeability alterations. Colistin stands as the main therapeutic option for ICU-acquired infections due to extensively drug-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii and should be considered as part of the empirical antibiotic regimen in settings with high densities of carbapenem-resistant strains. Stenotrophomonas maltophilia should be considered as naturally resistant to aminoglycosides, and the association of trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole with ticarcillin–clavulanate or fluoroquinolones is generally advocated as a first-line regimen for serious infections.
