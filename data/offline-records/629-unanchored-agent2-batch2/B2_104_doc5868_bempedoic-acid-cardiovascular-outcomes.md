## Synopsis (gist)

This review covers bempedoic acid in statin-intolerant patients, including cardiovascular outcomes and how the drug is used with other lipid-lowering therapy. The CLEAR Outcomes trial demonstrated that bempedoic acid lowers LDL-C and reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in statin-intolerant and/or unwilling patients with established ASCVD or at high cardiovascular risk, supporting its role as an effective treatment option. Bempedoic acid is an oral non-statin therapy activated in the liver, which limits exposure of skeletal muscle to the active drug and reduces the potential for muscle-related adverse effects. In practice, clinicians should assess the nocebo effect, distinguish partial versus complete statin intolerance, preserve any tolerated statin exposure, define the residual lipid-lowering gap, and align treatment intensity with cardiovascular risk and patient preferences. Clinicians should counsel and monitor for hyperuricemia, gout, and tendon injury, and bempedoic acid should be discontinued if tendinitis is suspected and most certainly if a tendon rupture occurs. Combination therapy should be initiated early when monotherapy is unlikely to reach the goal, while a sequential approach remains reasonable when the patient prefers it, with close reassessment and timely escalation recommended.
