## Synopsis (gist)

Orthotopic heart transplantation remains the main long-term treatment option for patients with advanced end-stage heart failure, and donor-tissue rejection most often occurs within the first months after surgery. Our findings suggest that the T2-mapping value may serve as a noninvasive imaging biomarker for transplant rejection, differentiating patients with and without histopathologically confirmed acute cellular rejection. Our study highlights that T2-mapping may serve as a supportive noninvasive imaging biomarker for identifying patients with a higher likelihood of clinically significant acute cellular rejection in heart-transplant recipients, particularly within the first 6 months post-surgery. The cutoff of 51 ms identified in this cohort reflects local acquisition conditions and should not be directly extrapolated to other centers without validation. While T1-mapping may contribute to tissue characterization in the transplant population, its utility in identifying acute rejection appears limited without corroborating evidence from T2-mapping or other imaging modalities.
