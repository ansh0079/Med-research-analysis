## Synopsis (gist)

Vocal cord dysfunction, also called inducible laryngeal obstruction, is characterized by breathlessness with inappropriate laryngeal narrowing, and the roundtable describes a heterogeneous presentation with diagnosis often delayed. Definitive diagnosis conventionally uses laryngoscopy showing inspiratory vocal fold narrowing greater than 50%, and laryngoscopy with provocation is called the gold standard. Dynamic computed tomography of the larynx is described as a possible faster test that still requires validation. The report states that there is no evidence-based standard of care because randomized treatment trials do not exist. Speech pathology is the mainstay first-line treatment, and botulinum toxin, inspiratory muscle strength training, and neuromodulators such as amitriptyline may be used with it or when it has not been effective. Multidisciplinary care is described as optimal, and early diagnosis is called paramount to avoid erroneous treatment.
