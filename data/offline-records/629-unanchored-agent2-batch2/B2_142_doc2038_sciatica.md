## Synopsis (gist)

The same Saudi GRADE guideline covers sciatica together with low back pain in adults and does not cover serious spinal pathology, inflammatory back pain with progressive neurological deficit, or suspected cauda equina syndrome. For sciatica, the Task Force suggests pharmacological treatment, a conditional recommendation with very low certainty of evidence, and does not recommend one particular medicine as first-line treatment. In the extract, NSAIDs have no recommendation for or against use, while benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids and other antiepileptics, and oral corticosteroids are recommended against. The Task Force suggests epidural injections for sciatica and suggests using image-concordant pathology or radicular symptoms to predict response to surgery, both conditional recommendations, and body mass index, smoking status, and psychological distress should not influence referral for a surgical opinion. Non-pharmacological and invasive options should be considered as the basis for managing sciatica where appropriate, and statements that cover low back pain and/or sciatica, including risk tools and return-to-work programmes, also apply here.
