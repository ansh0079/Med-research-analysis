## Synopsis (gist)

The International Stereotactic Radiosurgery Society presents consensus recommendations for stereotactic body radiotherapy of non-spine bone metastases, which the authors report can be delivered safely with low rates of adverse events and a very high local control rate. Simulation should use a CT scan with slice thickness of 3 mm or less, motion management should be considered for rib and sternal metastases, and PET may help localization but should not be relied on over CT and MRI when studies disagree. A CTV margin of 0–5 mm within contiguous bone is recommended when the GTV is based on MRI with or without PET/CT, and 5–10 mm when the GTV is based on CT alone, with the CTV kept to bone when the cortex is intact. Suggested schedules are 18–24 Gy in 1 fraction, 27–30 Gy in 3 fractions, and 30–35 Gy in 5 fractions, with de-escalation considered after previous radiotherapy, when organ-at-risk constraints cannot be met, or with moderate to severe cortical erosion. Dose escalation may be considered for radioresistant or oligometastatic disease, and site-specific statements address immobilization, fractionation, and respiratory motion for the shoulder girdle, ribs, sternum, and long bones.
