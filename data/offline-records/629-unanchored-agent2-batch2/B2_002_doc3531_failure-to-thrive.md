## Synopsis (gist)

A controlled trial randomised by primary care practice evaluated structured health visitor management, with dietetic, paediatric, and social work input as required, for failure to thrive in children under 2 years old in Newcastle upon Tyne. Of 229 children identified by population screening, 120 were in intervention practices and 109 in control practices, with follow up of records at age 3 years. In the families who consented to home visits, children in the intervention group were significantly heavier and taller and were reported to have better appetites than children in the control group. When the children were last weighed, 91 (76%) in the intervention group had recovered from failure to thrive compared with 60 (55%) in the control group. The trial concludes that health visitor intervention, with limited specialist support, can significantly improve growth compared with conventional management. Supporting health visitors in recognition and management also resulted in closer follow up and significantly better long term weight and height gain than conventional hospital based management, while 15-30% of control cases remained unrecognised and only 5% of cases seen at hospital had major organic disease.
