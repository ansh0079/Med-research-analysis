## Synopsis (gist)

The World Society of Emergency Surgery consensus guideline addresses management of sigmoid volvulus. Initial evaluation should include a focused history and physical examination and blood tests including blood gas and lactate, imaging starts with plain abdominal radiographs, and CT is used when the diagnosis is in doubt or ischemia or perforation is suspected. If ischemia or perforation is not suspected, flexible endoscopy should be performed first to decompress the colon, and urgent sigmoid resection is indicated when endoscopic detorsion is unsuccessful or the colon is non-viable or perforated. After successful endoscopic decompression, sigmoid colectomy should be offered as early as possible, including during the index admission, non-resectional procedures should be avoided, and endoscopic fixation may be considered when surgery presents a prohibitive risk. Patients with concomitant megacolon should undergo subtotal colectomy, colonic volvulus in pregnancy requires a multidisciplinary approach that accounts for the stage of pregnancy, and ileosigmoid volvulus most often requires surgical decompression.
