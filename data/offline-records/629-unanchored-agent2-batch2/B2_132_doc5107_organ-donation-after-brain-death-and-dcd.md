## Synopsis (gist)

The Bucharest ESOT consensus addresses paediatric controlled donation after circulatory determination of death (pcDCDD), described as the fastest growing form of deceased organ donation but still limited and uneven among children. Where feasible, any child aged 37 weeks CGA or older should be considered a possible pcDCDD donor regardless of weight, and suitability should be judged by a multi-disciplinary team rather than by one prediction of time to death after withdrawal of life sustaining medical treatment. Supportive treatments should be maintained until donation can be discussed, unless medical suitability is already excluded, and comfort care with sedatives and analgesia should follow usual end-of-life practice delivered by the treating team rather than organ donation staff. Donation discussions should where possible be led by a trained paediatric donation professional who is not involved in the child’s clinical care, and decisions about withdrawal should not be influenced by the possibility of organ donation. Where feasible, donation of paediatric hearts should be prioritised because of unmet need for heart transplantation and the difficulties of size matching, and local legislation, technology, and cultural norms should be weighed when pcDCDD programmes are introduced or expanded.
