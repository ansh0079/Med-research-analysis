## Synopsis (gist)

Corticosteroids remain the mainstay of therapy for noninfectious uveitis, and biologic agents have emerged as effective alternative treatments for individuals who are intolerant of or refractory to corticosteroids and conventional immunosuppressive therapies. Adalimumab was the first biologic approved by the Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of noninfectious uveitis. Screening for latent infections, including tuberculosis and hepatitis B and C viruses, is mandatory before treatment initiation, and live vaccines should be administered at least 4 weeks prior to initiating biologic treatment. Screening for pre-existing demyelinating disease is recommended prior to initiation, and close monitoring for new neurological symptoms during therapy is essential. IVIG should be considered only in cases of active, sight-threatening, noninfectious uveitis that are refractory to less expensive conventional treatments. A multidisciplinary approach involving a uveitis specialist and a rheumatologist is strongly recommended, especially in tuberculosis-endemic settings such as India.
