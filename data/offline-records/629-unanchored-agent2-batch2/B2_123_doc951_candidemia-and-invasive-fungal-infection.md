## Synopsis (gist)

Invasive fungal infections are a growing problem in critically ill patients and are associated with increased morbidity and mortality, and most of them are due to Candida species, especially Candida albicans. Timely diagnosis and proper treatment are of paramount importance, and factors related to the patient's clinical situation, comorbidities, local epidemiology, and the purpose of treatment should be taken into account when choosing an antifungal agent. Routine antifungal prophylaxis of all ICU patients is not recommended, while the review states that IDSA guidelines suggest fluconazole prophylaxis for high-risk ICU adults in hospitals with a high incidence of invasive candidiasis, and that empirical therapy should be considered for critically ill patients with risk factors and no other known cause of fever. For documented candidemia, the summarized guidelines suggest echinocandins or fluconazole according to clinical stability and recent azole exposure, a step-down from an echinocandin to fluconazole is suggested after clinical improvement and sterilization of blood cultures, and removal of central catheters is recommended by existing guidelines. Combination of amphotericin B with flucytosine is recommended in localized infection such as meningitis, osteomyelitis, and intra-abdominal infections. For invasive aspergillosis the treatment of choice is voriconazole, and for mucormycosis liposomal amphotericin B should be started, with posaconazole use recommended in combination with liposomal amphotericin B or as sequential long-term therapy.
