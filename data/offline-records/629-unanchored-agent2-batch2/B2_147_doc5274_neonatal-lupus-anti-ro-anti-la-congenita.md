## Synopsis (gist)

In the hydroxychloroquine section, many studies were confounded by pregnancy outcomes in patients with SLE who were also treated with other immunosuppressive agents, including use in anti-Ro/La positive patients in the prevention of congenital heart block. Despite those limitations, the text states that there were no appreciable adverse effects of HCQ on pregnancy duration or birth weight in the largest studies. Several comparisons of HCQ-treated and untreated cohorts with rheumatic disease, mostly SLE, either found no significant difference or found longer pregnancy duration and/or higher birth weight in the HCQ-treated pregnancies. The intravenous immunoglobulin review included pregnancies in which IVIG was used mostly in antiphospholipid syndrome or in the prevention of congenital heart block in anti-Ro/La positive mothers, and those studies focused on therapeutic efficacy rather than safety. Maternal and foetal complications were described as compatible with the underlying maternal disease rather than specific to IVIG, and the studies did not raise new concerns about the accepted safety of IVIG in pregnancy. The guideline states that IVIG is compatible with pregnancy (GRADE 1B, SOA 99.5%) and compatible with breastmilk exposure (GRADE 2C, SOA 100%).
