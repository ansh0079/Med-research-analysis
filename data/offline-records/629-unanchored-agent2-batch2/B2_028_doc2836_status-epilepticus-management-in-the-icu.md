## Synopsis (gist)

The claustrum sign is bilateral T2/FLAIR hyperintensity of the claustrum, first described in new-onset refractory status epilepticus and febrile infection-related epilepsy syndrome. In this systematic review, all but one patient with the claustrum sign met criteria for cryptogenic new-onset refractory status epilepticus, patients were predominantly young, and a febrile prodrome was present in 93% of cases. Convulsive status epilepticus occurred in 98%, progressing to super-refractory status epilepticus in more than half of reported cases, and immunotherapy was administered in 92% of patients. In-hospital mortality was 9%, while 74% of patients developed chronic epilepsy. Compared with patients without claustrum involvement, those with the claustrum sign had lower rates of super-refractory status epilepticus and intensive care admission, and a trend toward lower mortality. Recognition of the claustrum sign may support early diagnosis and targeted treatment, and early recognition may assist diagnostic stratification, inform therapeutic decision-making, and prompt timely immunomodulatory intervention.
