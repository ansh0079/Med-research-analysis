**Fit:** partly

## Synopsis (gist)

This review describes distal radial access technique, pitfalls, and recommendations in interventional cardiology, presenting it as an alternative to traditional radial and femoral access. It recommends ultrasound assessment of snuffbox anatomy and of the distal radial artery before puncture, a minimum luminal diameter of approximately 1.8 mm for coronary catheters, and hydrophilic coated thin-walled sheaths. Ultrasound-guided puncture, 21-gauge needles, and avoidance of routine skin incision are recommended, and dedicated hemostatic devices are recommended to maintain distal flow and reduce radial artery occlusion. Adequate anticoagulation should not be avoided, and if the guidewire cannot be advanced, manual compression and bandaging should be used to prevent compartment syndrome. For larger hematomas, intermittent proximal compression should be considered while monitoring distal perfusion, and surgical management should be considered if compartment syndrome develops.
