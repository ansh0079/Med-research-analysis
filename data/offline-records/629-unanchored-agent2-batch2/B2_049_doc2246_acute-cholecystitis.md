## Synopsis (gist)

This Delphi consensus on percutaneous cholecystostomy in acute cholecystitis reached agreement on only six of twenty-seven questions. When cholecystostomy is clearly indicated, the panel agreed it is not necessary to wait 48 hours. Surgery is the first therapeutic option for Tokyo Guidelines grade II acute cholecystitis in a patient suitable for surgery, and there is no indication for percutaneous cholecystostomy in grade I. The transhepatic route is the route of choice, cholangiography should be done before drain removal, and laparoscopic cholecystectomy is the preferred approach after cholecystostomy. For grade III disease, the discussion states that cholecystostomy should not be considered the first option if the surgeon finds the patient fit for surgery. A clear timing for the drain could not be recommended.

## Hand-back notes

The source is an international Delphi consensus limited to percutaneous cholecystostomy in acute cholecystitis. Antibiotic regimens and the broader operative pathway for acute cholecystitis, beyond the statements on who should have surgery rather than cholecystostomy, are outside this consensus. Sections excluded: Delphi methods, round statistics that did not reach the 70% threshold, and reference-list titles were not listed as recommendations. The normal-cholangiogram removal item passed round 1 but not round 2 and was not a final recommendation.
