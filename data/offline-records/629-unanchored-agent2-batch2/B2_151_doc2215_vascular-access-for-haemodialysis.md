## Synopsis (gist)

We recommend focused access advice for adults and children anticipating or undergoing haemodialysis, and we recommend treating access choice as a patient decision supported by the multidisciplinary team. We suggest access referral, and fistula formation if suitable, when haemodialysis is likely to start within 12 months, while vascular access education is appropriate at any stage of kidney disease. We recommend favouring distal sites first when anatomy and preference allow, counselling patients to expect a poorer outcome if a fistula vessel is under 2.0 mm, and favouring fistula formation over a graft in adults and teenage children except when early cannulation is needed or anatomy is unfavourable. New fistulas should be monitored for maturation, cannulation should follow an individualised assessment, and an access assessment using look, feel, and listen is recommended before every cannulation, with rope-ladder or buttonhole cannulation preferred to area puncture. For catheters, we recommend the right internal jugular vein as the usual site, avoiding the subclavian route when another vein is available, and using real-time ultrasound, with fluoroscopy for left-sided or subclavian approaches.
