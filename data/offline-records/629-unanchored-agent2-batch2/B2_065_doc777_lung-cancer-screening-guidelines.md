## Synopsis (gist)

The NELSON randomized trial assigned high-risk former and current smokers to volume CT screening at baseline, year 1, year 3, and year 5.5 or to no screening. Among men, the cumulative rate ratio for death from lung cancer at 10 years was 0.76 in the screening group compared with the control group. Lung-cancer mortality was significantly lower among participants who underwent volume CT screening than among those who underwent no screening, and follow-up procedures for results suggestive of lung cancer were uncommon. Volume CT screening was also associated with a shift toward lower-stage cancers at diagnosis and more frequent eligibility for curative treatment, mainly surgical. Trial data suggest greater benefits in women than in men, but that comparison used a relatively small number of women, and the authors state that more research is required in women and in other subgroups.

## Hand-back notes

The source is the NELSON randomized trial of volume CT screening, not a lung-cancer screening guideline. It reports mortality, stage shift, and follow-up rates in the trial population and does not issue population eligibility rules, routine screening intervals, or shared decision-making recommendations outside that protocol.
