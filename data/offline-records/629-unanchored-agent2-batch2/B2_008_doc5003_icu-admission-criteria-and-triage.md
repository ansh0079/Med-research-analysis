## Synopsis (gist)

This single center prospective study evaluated consecutive patients referred for ICU admission during overcrowding in April and May 2014. Of 95 patients, the mean APACHE-II score was 16.8, 64 (67.4%) were accepted to the ICU, 18 (18.9%) were triaged to a step-down unit, and 13 (13.7%) were admitted to hospital wards. At this institution, triage decisions were not related to the number of available ICU beds, age, or gender, and APACHE-II score correlated with the likelihood of ICU admission. A total of 17 patients (17.9%) died in the hospital, and 15 (23.4%) expired in ICU. Alternative locations outside the ICU should be considered during extreme ICU-bed shortage, and step-down units, intermediate care units, and PACUs might be acceptable for critical care when ICU beds are constrained. Triage strategies should comply with ethical principles so that patients have equal access to ICU management regardless of personal and behavioral characteristics.
