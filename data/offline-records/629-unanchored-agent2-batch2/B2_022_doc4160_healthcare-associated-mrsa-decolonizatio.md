## Synopsis (gist)

The source reviews decolonization and pathogen reduction to prevent antimicrobial-resistant healthcare-associated infections, including infections related to Staphylococcus aureus. Nasal carriage of Staphylococcus aureus is described as a major risk factor for wound infection, surgical-site infection, or bloodstream infection, and MRSA is described as carrying substantial infection risk. CDC guidance for preventing MRSA infections is stated to include decolonization and pathogen reduction, including intranasal mupirocin and chlorhexidine bathing. The REDUCE MRSA trial is cited as reporting fewer MRSA-positive clinical cultures and fewer bloodstream infections from any pathogen with universal decolonization using chlorhexidine bathing in intensive care. Available evidence suggests that a universal approach is more effective for reducing bloodstream infections from any pathogen than targeted pathogen reduction limited to Staphylococcus aureus-colonized patients. The authors conclude that approaches to pathogen reduction will be multifaceted and that the role of the microbiome in colonization resistance should be considered for all decolonization strategies.

## Hand-back notes

The source reviews pathogen reduction and decolonization to prevent antimicrobial-resistant healthcare-associated infections, with MRSA nasal decolonization and chlorhexidine bathing as one major example. It is not limited to MRSA and does not set out a complete MRSA screening, contact-precaution, or infection-treatment protocol.
