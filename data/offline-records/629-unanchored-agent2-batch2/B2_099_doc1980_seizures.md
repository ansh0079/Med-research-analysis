## Synopsis (gist)

The ILAE Task Force on Neonatal Seizures developed evidence-based and consensus-based recommendations about antiseizure medication management in neonates. In neonates with seizures requiring ASM, phenobarbital should be the first-line ASM regardless of etiology, including HIE, stroke, and hemorrhage. If channelopathy is the likely cause because of family history, phenytoin or carbamazepine may be the first-line ASM. Phenytoin, levetiracetam, midazolam, or lidocaine may be considered as second-line ASM for most etiologies, and levetiracetam should be preferred when cardiac disorders are present. Following cessation of acute provoked seizures without evidence for neonatal-onset epilepsy, ASM should be discontinued before discharge, regardless of MRI or EEG findings. Therapeutic hypothermia may reduce seizure burden in hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, and a trial of pyridoxine may be attempted when vitamin B6-dependent epilepsy is suspected or seizures do not respond to second-line ASM.
