## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review covers the epidemiology, humanistic burden, current treatment, and economic burden of chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy. Cited EFNS/PNS guidance recommends intravenous immunoglobulin or corticosteroids for moderate to severe disability, plasma exchange if those are ineffective, and an immunosuppressant or immunomodulator combination when response is inadequate or the maintenance dose is high. That guidance is reported as providing no recommendations on long-term management because of lack of evidence. Studies suggest subcutaneous immunoglobulin has similar efficacy to intravenous immunoglobulin and that patients prefer it because of fewer adverse events and greater independence. Treatment selection needs to consider severity, health status, tolerability, and contraindications, and it is recommended that intravenous immunoglobulin be periodically reduced or withdrawn to avoid excessive costs while corticosteroids are reviewed or withdrawn to avoid adverse events.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a burden-of-illness review that summarizes published CIDP treatments, including EFNS/PNS recommendations, rather than an original graded treatment guideline. Dosing schedules and a complete long-term management algorithm are out of scope; the 2010 EFNS/PNS guidelines are reported as not addressing long-term management or subcutaneous immunoglobulin.
