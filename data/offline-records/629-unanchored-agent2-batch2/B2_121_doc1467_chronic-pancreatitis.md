## Synopsis (gist)

The ESPEN practical guideline states nutrition recommendations for chronic pancreatitis, in which risk of malnutrition is high and malnutrition is common. Patients with chronic pancreatitis should be considered at risk, screened and supplemented accordingly, and osteoporosis and increased facture risk should be acknowledged, with preventive measures considered. When pancreatic exocrine insufficiency is diagnosed, pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy shall be initiated, pH-sensitive enteric-coated microsphere preparations shall be used, and oral pancreatic enzymes should be distributed along with meals and snacks. Patients with chronic pancreatitis do not need to follow a restrictive diet, those with a normal nutritional status should adhere to a well-balanced diet, diets very high in fiber should be avoided, and there is no need for dietary fat restriction unless symptoms of steatorrhea cannot be controlled. Malnourished patients should be advised to consume high protein, high-energy food in five to six small meals per day, with oral nutritional supplements, enteral nutrition, or parenteral nutrition used when oral intake does not meet goals. Patients should undergo screening for micro- and macronutrient deficiencies at least every twelve months, and basic preventive measures for bone health should be advised to all patients with chronic pancreatitis.
