## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review assessed paired inspiratory and expiratory high-resolution CT as an added tool for the differential diagnosis of cystic lung lesions. Three studies met inclusion criteria, comprising 149 participants and 513 cystic lesions. Pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis, lymphangioleiomyomatosis, honeycombing, and cystic bronchiectasis became considerably smaller on expiratory CT, while the size of emphysema tended to remain constant through the respiratory cycle. In one included study, lymphangioleiomyomatosis showed an average diameter reduction of 42.8%, from 4.9 mm during inspiration to 2.8 mm during expiration. The authors suggest that paired inspiratory and expiratory CT can help differentiate emphysema from other diseases with a cystic pattern because the scans show dynamic properties of the lesions. They also state that expiratory CT can serve as a valuable adjunct in that differential diagnosis, and that average cyst-size reduction alone, as used in the included studies, is not sufficient for further refining diagnostics.
