## Synopsis (gist)

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists are a well-established treatment option for the management of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. Spironolactone has been used for HFrEF for more than 2 decades following the RALES trial, which led to approval for patients with HFrEF and NYHA class III-IV symptoms. Treatment with an sMRA for HFrEF should not be initiated if serum potassium is >5.0 mEq/L for spironolactone or >5.5 mEq/L for eplerenone, and people with HFrEF and an eGFR >50 mL/min/1.73 m2 should begin spironolactone at 25 mg once daily. The daily dose of spironolactone may be increased to 50 mg if 25 mg is well tolerated and a dose increase is clinically indicated. ESC and AHA/ACC/HFSA guidelines state that treatment with an sMRA may be considered in patients with HFmrEF (Class IIb). Serum potassium should be measured within 1 week of spironolactone initiation or titration and regularly thereafter, and if hyperkalemia occurs the dose should be decreased or discontinued while the hyperkalemia is treated.
