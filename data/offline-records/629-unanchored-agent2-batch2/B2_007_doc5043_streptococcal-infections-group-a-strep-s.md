## Synopsis (gist)

In 2022 the Netherlands observed a sevenfold increase in notifiable invasive Streptococcus pyogenes infections among children aged 0–5 years compared with pre-COVID-19 pandemic years. Of 42 cases in that age group, seven had preceding or coinciding varicella zoster infection and nine were fatal, and the increase was not attributable to a specific emm type. Notifications of non-puerperal invasive disease, defined as streptococcal toxic shock syndrome or necrotising fasciitis, were markedly increased in 2022 compared with 2016–19. Vigilance for clinical deterioration as a sign of invasive infection is warranted in young children, especially those with varicella zoster infection. The ongoing increase, severity, and case fatality warrant awareness among clinicians, public health officials, and parents. Clinical deterioration in children with chickenpox or respiratory viral infection should raise suspicion of Streptococcus pyogenes superinfection warranting early antibiotic treatment and, where indicated, hospital admission.
