## Synopsis (gist)

This SCAI expert consensus addresses management of patients with STEMI referred for primary PCI, including catheterization laboratory readiness, arterial access, diagnostic angiography, thrombus, no-reflow, imaging, and special anatomical and clinical circumstances. Timely reperfusion with primary percutaneous coronary intervention is associated with improved outcomes, and when primary PCI cannot be performed within 120 minutes of first medical contact, fibrinolytic therapy is recommended. Transradial access is the preferred route for coronary angiography and PCI, a complete diagnostic coronary angiogram should be performed during the index procedure, and measurement of LVEDP can help guide further treatment. Angiographic assessment of thrombus burden should be made after the wire crosses the lesion, bail-out aspiration thrombectomy in selected cases of large thrombus is an acceptable strategy, and routine use of intracoronary imaging is encouraged to guide PCI. RHC should be performed in STEMI with CS during the index procedure, and complete revascularization of significant non-infarct stenosis should be performed in patients with multivessel disease. If fibrinolytic therapy fails, immediate catheterization and rescue PCI are essential, and in stable patients early catheterization within 24 hours of fibrinolytic therapy is indicated.
