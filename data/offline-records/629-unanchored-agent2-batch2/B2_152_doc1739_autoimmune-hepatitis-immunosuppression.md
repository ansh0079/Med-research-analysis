## Synopsis (gist)

We recommend immunosuppressive treatment in most patients with autoimmune hepatitis, regardless of symptoms, with high grade of evidence and a strong recommendation. In mild autoimmune hepatitis, defined in the text by ALT below 50 U/L, Ishak necroinflammatory score below 6, and fibrosis stage below 2, the decision to offer treatment should be based on benefits and risks. The initial prednisolone dose is recommended at about 0.5 mg/kg/day and not usually above 40 mg/day, then reduced over 1 to 3 months to 5–10 mg/day, while azathioprine can be increased from 1 to 2 mg/kg/day as the steroid dose falls. Mycophenolate mofetil is recommended as an option for patients who are intolerant of azathioprine and who are taking active steps not to conceive. For overlap syndromes with severe interface hepatitis or bridging necrosis, immunosuppression is recommended at diagnosis, and combination therapy with ursodeoxycholic acid plus prednisolone and azathioprine might give the best biochemical response.
