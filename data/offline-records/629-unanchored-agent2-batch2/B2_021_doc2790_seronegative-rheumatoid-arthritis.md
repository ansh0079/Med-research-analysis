## Synopsis (gist)

Seronegative rheumatoid arthritis is presented as a form of rheumatoid arthritis in which autoantibodies are missing, and the review aims to help clinicians diagnose it as accurately as possible. The review states that if distinctive features are not recognized, seronegative disease can be underdiagnosed and the window of opportunity for early diagnosis and timely treatment can be missed. According to the ACR/EULAR recommendations cited in the source, the initial therapy of rheumatoid arthritis should be based on conventional synthetic antirheumatic drugs. The first-line conventional synthetic drug suggested by most experts, regardless of serologic status, is methotrexate, although several studies are described as showing that methotrexate is less effective in seronegative than in seropositive disease. Several lines of evidence suggest that seronegative rheumatoid arthritis is, in general, more responsive to therapy than seropositive disease, with a more favorable long-term radiographic outcome. The authors note that randomized clinical trials specific to seronegative rheumatoid arthritis have not yet been conducted.
