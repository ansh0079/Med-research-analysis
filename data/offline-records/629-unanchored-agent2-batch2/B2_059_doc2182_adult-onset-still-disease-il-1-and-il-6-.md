**Fit:** full

## Synopsis (gist)

A Dutch Delphi panel reached consensus on diagnostic and treatment statements for adult-onset Still disease, for which the authors state there is no internationally accepted treatment guideline. Corticosteroids are effective but the aim should be to limit their frequency and duration because of side effects, and early initiation of biologics is preferred over conventional treatment such as methotrexate. IL-1 blockade is described as an effective treatment early in the disease course, including in glucocorticoid-naive patients, and IL-6 blockade is a treatment option in glucocorticoid-resistant or glucocorticoid-dependent disease. For severe systemic disease the stated options are IL-1 blockade, glucocorticoids plus IL-1 blockade, or glucocorticoid monotherapy, and step-up to IL-1 blockade should be considered if glucocorticoid monotherapy is insufficient or dependence develops, while arthritis-predominant disease may use methotrexate plus glucocorticoids, IL-6 blockade, or IL-1 blockade. When the diagnosis is considered, standard laboratory tests, ferritin, and Yamaguchi criteria are required, soluble IL-2 receptor or IL-18 and PET/CT should be considered before glucocorticoids, and bone marrow examination for suspected macrophage activation syndrome should not delay treatment.
