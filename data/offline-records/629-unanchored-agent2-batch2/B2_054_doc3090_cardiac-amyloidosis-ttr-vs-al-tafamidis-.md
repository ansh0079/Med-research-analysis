## Synopsis (gist)

Cardiac amyloidosis, including transthyretin and light-chain subtypes, is described as an underrecognized mimic of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, and the role of SGLT2 inhibitors in this population is unclear. In a meta-analysis of seven observational studies, SGLT2 inhibitor use in the primary ATTR analysis was associated with lower observed all-cause mortality and, among secondary outcomes, reduced NT-proBNP and improved eGFR. An exploratory analysis combining AL and ATTR had substantial between-study heterogeneity and should not be interpreted as a single class effect, and AL mortality data were too limited to support a conclusion or to extrapolate ATTR findings to AL. The authors suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors appear generally well-tolerated in both ATTR and AL cardiac amyloidosis, with safety observations described as descriptive only and to be interpreted with caution. Because the included studies were observational, the findings are hypothesis-generating, should not be interpreted as evidence of efficacy, and do not constitute a treatment recommendation. Randomized trials are required before SGLT2 inhibitors can be routinely recommended in cardiac amyloidosis, particularly in AL, where the evidence base remains sparse.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a systematic review and meta-analysis of SGLT2 inhibitors in ATTR and AL cardiac amyloidosis. Diagnosis of cardiac amyloidosis, bone scintigraphy, and tafamidis treatment recommendations are out of scope. Tafamidis is mentioned only as concurrent disease-modifying therapy in included observational studies, and the authors state that observed associations are inseparable from that concurrent therapy.
