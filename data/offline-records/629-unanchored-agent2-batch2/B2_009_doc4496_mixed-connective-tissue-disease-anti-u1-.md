## Synopsis (gist)

Mixed connective tissue disease is described as an overlap disease with features of systemic sclerosis, poly/dermatomyositis, and systemic lupus erythematous in patients with antibodies targeting the U1 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein particle. There are no generally accepted classification criteria, and the early description of a benign disease without organ involvement and with a prompt response to low-dose glucocorticoids has not been confirmed in all studies. A systematic search identified no clinical practice guideline specific to mixed connective tissue disease. Five references developed for other connective tissue diseases were selected as usable in this context, including testing of anti-Sm and anti-RNP antibodies, screening for pulmonary arterial hypertension, antinuclear antibody assessment, skin ulcer management, and cardiovascular MR. There is no agreement on initial or long-term treatment, no randomised controlled trial is identified, and patients are usually treated by similarity to other connective tissue diseases or by organ-based management. The worst prognosis and high mortality are associated with pulmonary disease, and early targeted intervention is stated to be associated with a better outcome.
