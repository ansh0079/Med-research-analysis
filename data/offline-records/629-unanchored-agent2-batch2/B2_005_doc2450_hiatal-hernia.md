## Synopsis (gist)

This editorial discusses the SAGES guidelines for the management of hiatal hernia, which use the GRADE system and provide 28 recommendations spanning diagnosis, surgical technique, and perioperative care, including pediatric and bariatric populations. The editorial emphasizes that the document is a guideline for hiatal hernia, and that gastroesophageal reflux disease overlaps with it but is a different condition. Heartburn and regurgitation are stated to be among the most common symptoms in patients with paraesophageal hernia. Anemia is often attributed to mechanical trauma at the diaphragm, including Cameron ulcers or occult erosions, generally with normal mucosal blood supply. Fundoplication may be optional in some patients with paraesophageal hernia, particularly in urgent or emergent circumstances, while the standard of care is to include fundoplication in the repair of sliding hernias. Preoperative dysphagia is called the single most important risk factor for dysphagia after surgery, and the editorial states that both laparoscopic and open revisional surgery might reasonably be considered appropriate, with the causes of failure sought.
