## Synopsis (gist)

The 2012 American College of Rheumatology Part 1 guideline addresses nonpharmacologic and pharmacologic urate-lowering therapy for gout, including chronic tophaceous gouty arthropathy among the case scenarios. The Task Force Panel recommended diet and lifestyle measures for the vast majority of patients, including limiting purine-rich meat and seafood, limiting high fructose corn syrup-sweetened drinks, encouraging low-fat or non-fat dairy, reducing alcohol, and abstinence from alcohol during active arthritis, especially in chronic tophaceous gouty arthropathy. Xanthine oxidase inhibitor therapy with either allopurinol or febuxostat is recommended as the first-line pharmacologic approach, with a serum urate target below 6 mg/dL at a minimum, and lowering below 5 mg/dL may be needed to improve signs and symptoms including tophi. The starting dose of allopurinol should be no greater than 100 mg per day, lower in stage 4 or worse chronic kidney disease, with gradual upward titration that can exceed 300 mg daily, and HLA-B*5801 testing should be considered before allopurinol in selected high-risk populations. Probenecid was recommended as an alternative first-line option if a xanthine oxidase inhibitor cannot be used, but it is not recommended as first-line monotherapy when creatinine clearance is below 50 ml/minute, and a history of urolithiasis contraindicates first-line uricosuric monotherapy. When the serum urate target is not met, the panel recommended upward titration or substitution of a xanthine oxidase inhibitor and combination with a uricosuric, and pegloticase is appropriate only for severe disease burden with refractoriness or intolerance to appropriately dosed oral therapy, not as first-line therapy.

## Hand-back notes

The source is the 2012 ACR gout guideline Part 1 on nonpharmacologic and pharmacologic urate-lowering therapy. Chronic tophaceous gouty arthropathy is one of the case scenarios, and the recommendations also cover other gout phenotypes. Acute gout attack treatment is outside this Part 1 document.
