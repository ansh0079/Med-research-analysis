## Synopsis (gist)

The APAGE clinical practice guideline concerns small molecules and IL-23 p19 inhibitors in ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, and it includes graded statements on pregnancy and breastfeeding rather than a complete IBD-in-pregnancy guideline. IL-23 p19 inhibitors (guselkumab, mirikizumab, and risankizumab) can safely be used in IBD patients who are pregnant or attempting conception and can safely be used during breastfeeding, with level of agreement 100%, quality of evidence III, and classification of recommendation C. JAK inhibitors (filgotinib, tofacitinib, and upadacitinib) should be avoided in IBD patients who are considering pregnancy and breastfeeding, classified as recommendation C. S1P modulators (etrasimod and ozanimod) should be avoided in patients who are considering pregnancy and breastfeeding. The text also reports that other groups recommend avoiding JAK inhibitors in pregnancy, including discontinuing a JAK inhibitor 4 weeks before conception, and that for ozanimod one guideline recommends stopping 6 months before conception while another suggests stopping 3 months pre-conception. A separate passage states that insufficient data exist for definitive recommendations on some pregnancy safety questions and that caution remains advised pending more comprehensive pregnancy safety studies.
