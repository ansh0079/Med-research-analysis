## Synopsis (gist)

Repeated Clostridioides difficile infection is described as difficult to stop with usual antibiotic treatment, and fecal microbiota transplantation is examined as a way to restore gut microbial balance. This review compared fecal microbiota transplantation with standard antibiotic therapy across fifteen studies. Fecal microbiota transplantation worked better than antibiotics, with lower recurrence, and the benefit was reported for colonoscopy, tube, and capsule delivery. Side effects were usually small and about the same for fecal microbiota transplantation and antibiotics. In conclusion, the authors state that fecal microbiota transplantation is safer and does a better job than normal antibiotics for recurrent infection and should be thought of as the main treatment after the first recurrence. The review also states that fecal microbiota transplantation is very effective and secure for recurrent infection and helps update present treatment.
