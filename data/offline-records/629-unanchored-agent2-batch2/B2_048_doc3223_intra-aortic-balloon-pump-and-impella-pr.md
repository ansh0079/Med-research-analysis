## Synopsis (gist)

Cardiogenic shock is described as a major cause of morbidity and mortality, and the intra-aortic balloon pump is presented as the most widely used short-term support device, with only modest support. The latest European Society of Cardiology guidelines are cited as no longer recommending routine intra-aortic balloon pump placement in acute myocardial infarction cardiogenic shock, class III. Impella is described as providing forward flow that unloads the left ventricle. A metallic aortic valve precludes Impella, severe peripheral vascular disease contraindicates femoral cannulation, and Impella should be avoided when anticoagulation cannot be tolerated. Removal with embolectomy should be considered for acute limb ischemia, and hemolysis should be considered when hemoglobin falls without explanation. When the hemodynamic profile can be assessed, the review recommends choosing the device that fits the physiological derangement, and it advises documenting the goal of support before initiation and avoiding premature use.
