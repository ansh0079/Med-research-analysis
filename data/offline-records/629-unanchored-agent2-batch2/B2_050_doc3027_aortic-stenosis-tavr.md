## Synopsis (gist)

This meta-analysis compares redo transcatheter aortic valve implantation with surgical explantation after failure of a transcatheter heart valve. The abstract reports lower in-hospital, 30-day, and 1-year mortality with redo implantation, along with less major bleeding, fewer 30-day readmissions, and shorter hospitalization, and a higher risk of greater-than-mild paravalvular leak. A later summary states that redo implantation shortened hospital stay, while several other events including major bleeding and 30-day readmission were similar, and that explantation reduced greater-than-mild paravalvular leak. In anatomically suitable patients without features such as infective endocarditis, severe prosthesis-patient mismatch, or unfavorable root anatomy, redo implantation should be considered the preferred reintervention because of lower early mortality and shorter hospitalization. The closing statement says redo implantation should be preferred in anatomically suitable patients, while explantation remains an option when redo implantation is contraindicated or unlikely to be durable or safe, including infective endocarditis, severe prosthesis-patient mismatch, unfavorable root anatomy, or high coronary risk. The authors note that wide confidence intervals and heterogeneity mean these findings should be incorporated carefully, and that outcomes of explantation may improve with experience.
