## Synopsis (gist)

Residual symptoms after treatment of Lyme disease, called post-treatment Lyme disease symptoms, were reviewed to guide treatment recommendations. Eligible randomized trials showed no statistically significant difference between antibiotics and placebo for quality of life, cognition, and depression, and studies with low risk of bias showed no significant difference for fatigue. A meta-analysis of safety outcomes showed statistically significantly more adverse events for antibiotics compared with placebo. The available literature is heterogeneous, but overall shows evidence of no effect of antibiotics on quality of life, depression, cognition, and fatigue, while showing more adverse events. Patients with suspected post-treatment Lyme disease symptoms should not be treated with antibiotics, and the review states that antibiotic treatment should not be used in these patients. Symptom-specific interdisciplinary approaches regarding persistent symptoms may be promising in the management of patients with persistent symptoms after Lyme disease.
