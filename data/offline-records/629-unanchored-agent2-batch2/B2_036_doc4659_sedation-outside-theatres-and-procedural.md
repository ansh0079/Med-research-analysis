## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated intravenous esketamine as an adjuvant for sedation or analgesia outside the operating room in adults and children. In adults, intravenous esketamine combinations were significantly associated with a decreased risk of oxygen desaturation, hypotension, bradycardia, injection pain, and body movement, and with lower propofol consumption, but with an increased risk of psychiatric symptoms. Subgroup analysis showed that only the combination of esketamine and propofol significantly reduced those respiratory and cardiovascular adverse events in adults. In children, the combined use of esketamine and propofol may cause a lower risk of hypotension when esketamine is above 0.5 mg/kg, but a higher risk of visual disturbance and dizziness at that dose. Intravenous esketamine, particularly in combination with propofol, may improve the safety and efficacy of sedation and analgesia outside the operating room, although clinicians should be aware of the potential psychiatric symptoms of esketamine.
