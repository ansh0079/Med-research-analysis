## Synopsis (gist)

The six-minute walk test is described as a measure of functional capacity and as an accepted alternative end point in cardiomyopathy trials, including transthyretin-mediated amyloid cardiomyopathy. Across 41 studies, a greater walk distance was associated with a reduced risk of hospitalization or mortality in cardiomyopathy. In transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, a greater baseline distance or a smaller decrease over time was associated with a reduced risk of cardiovascular hospitalization and mortality. The authors suggest that the test is a valid measure of cardiomyopathy severity and support its use as a key assessment in cardiomyopathy trials. Cited guidelines support its use, together with other measures, to monitor progression and treatment response in hereditary transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, and they treat a decline in distance as reflective of disease progression. The prognostic value may be reduced when the diagnosis is made earlier and severe cardiovascular impairment is not yet present.
