## Synopsis (gist)

Results of randomized controlled trials are consistent in showing reduced rates of stroke, heart failure and cardiovascular events in very old patients treated with antihypertensive drugs, although inconsistencies exist with regard to the effect of these drugs on total mortality. Treating hypertension in very old patients reduces stroke and heart failure with no effect on total mortality. The meta-regression suggested that a reduction in mortality was achieved in trials with the least blood pressure reductions and the lowest intensity of therapy. The results are compelling enough to recommend a low-maximal intensity of antihypertensive therapy in individuals 80 years and over. The most reasonable strategy is the one associated with significant mortality reduction; thiazides as first-line drugs with a maximum of two drugs. In patients over 80 years old the strategy proposed by the hypertension in the very elderly trial is a thiazide diuretic as first-line therapy and maximal antihypertensive therapy with two drugs in low doses.
