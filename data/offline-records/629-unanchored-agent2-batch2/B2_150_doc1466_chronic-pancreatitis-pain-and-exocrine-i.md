## Synopsis (gist)

The only graded statements visible in this extract address pancreatic exocrine insufficiency after acute pancreatitis, not chronic pancreatitis pain. The text says that about 62% of patients develop exocrine insufficiency on admission with acute pancreatitis and about one third still have it during follow-up, which is used to support screening, especially after severe necrotizing disease or alcoholic etiology. Statement 5.5.1 says no delay is recommended in confirming the diagnosis of pancreatic exocrine insufficiency after recovery from acute pancreatitis. Statement 5.5.2 recommends rescreening for exocrine insufficiency during follow-up in patients who had it after acute pancreatitis, in order to re-evaluate the need to maintain pancreatic enzyme replacement. Agreement with those statements is given as 87.5% and 90.3%, with level of evidence 5. Chronic pancreatitis pain recommendations are not in this extract.
