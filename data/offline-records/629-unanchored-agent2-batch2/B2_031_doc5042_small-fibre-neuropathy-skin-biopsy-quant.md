## Synopsis (gist)

Definitive diagnostic criteria for small fibre neuropathy have yet to be established, with implications for clinical practice and for the design of clinical trials. This reappraisal and validation study based criteria on deep clinical phenotyping, quantitative sensory testing, and intraepidermal nerve fibre density. The combination of clinical signs and abnormal quantitative sensory testing and/or intraepidermal nerve fibre density findings can more reliably lead to the diagnosis than abnormal quantitative sensory testing and intraepidermal nerve fibre density without clinical signs. Sensory symptoms alone should not be considered a reliable screening feature, and patients should be suspected to have small fibre neuropathy when at least two clinical signs are present. Quantification of intraepidermal nerve fibre density remains the most reliable tool to confirm the diagnosis, and assessment of small fibre nerves should be in the frame of a well-defined clinical context. The combination of various somatic and autonomic nerve testing has been proposed to increase diagnostic ability, but none of them has been validated or concretely applied in clinical practice.
