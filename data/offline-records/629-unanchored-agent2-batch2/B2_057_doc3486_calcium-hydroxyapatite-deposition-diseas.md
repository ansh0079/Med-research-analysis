## Synopsis (gist)

Hydroxyapatite deposition disease is characterized by accumulation of hydroxyapatite crystals in soft tissues with subsequent inflammation, and this review covers proposed mechanisms, stages around calcification, imaging findings, and differential diagnosis. Radiography, CT, MRI, and ultrasound are used to identify the calcific deposits, judge extent, and distinguish the disease from conditions with similar imaging findings. First-line management often consists of rest, physical therapy, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, chosen with attention to severity, deposit location, and overall health. If conservative treatment does not give sufficient relief, extracorporeal shock wave therapy and surgical removal of deposits are considered, and surgery is typically reserved for severe cases after weighing health status, symptom severity, and response to conservative care. Imaging specialists may perform image-guided needle aspiration or lavage and guide corticosteroid injections, and the treatment plan is described as a blend of conservative and interventionist approaches tailored to the patient.
