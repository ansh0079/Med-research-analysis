## Synopsis (gist)

Five overarching principles and six treat-to-target recommendations were formulated for giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica and are meant to be understood as complementary to current international treatment recommendations. GCA is a medical emergency because of the imminent risk of sight loss and other ischaemic events and therefore requires immediate treatment, and treatment of a person with high suspicion for GCA should not be delayed because of pending diagnostic procedures. Glucocorticoids are the standard treatment for GCA and PMR. The treatment target should be remission, defined as the absence of clinical symptoms and systemic inflammation, and treatment of GCA should also aim to prevent tissue ischaemia and vascular damage. Relapses may be present despite normal ESR and CRP, particularly in people treated with interleukin-6 receptor blocking agents, and this should not be understood as a recommendation to reach negative acute phase reactants at all costs. Disease activity should be monitored regularly, comorbidities may influence assessment of the treatment target and should be considered before modifying treatment, and once remission is reached it should be maintained with the minimal effective dose of medication.

## Hand-back notes

The document is a treat-to-target recommendation set for giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica. Diagnostic use of ESR and CRP, temporal artery ultrasound, and high-dose glucocorticoid induction regimens are out of scope. ESR and CRP are discussed only as imperfect markers of systemic inflammation, including that relapses may occur despite normal values and that interleukin-6 receptor blockade makes them unreliable. Temporal artery ultrasound is not in the recommendation text. No high-dose steroid dose is specified; glucocorticoids are called the standard treatment. Sections excluded: Methods of the systematic literature review and level-of-agreement statistics were not copied as recommendations. PMR is included only where the stated recommendation also names GCA or applies to both diseases.
