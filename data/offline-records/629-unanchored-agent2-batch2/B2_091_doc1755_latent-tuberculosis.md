## Synopsis (gist)

The 2020 National Tuberculosis Controllers Association and CDC guidelines update treatment of latent tuberculosis infection for persons living in the United States using GRADE. Three preferred rifamycin-based regimens are 3 months of once-weekly isoniazid plus rifapentine, strongly recommended for adults and children older than 2 years including HIV-positive persons as drug interactions allow; 4 months of daily rifampin, strongly recommended for HIV-negative adults and children of all ages; and 3 months of daily isoniazid plus rifampin, conditionally recommended for adults and children of all ages and for HIV-positive persons as drug interactions allow. Regimens of 6 or 9 months of daily isoniazid are alternative recommended regimens; 6 months daily is strongly recommended for HIV-negative adults and children and conditionally for HIV-positive persons, and 9 months daily is conditionally recommended regardless of HIV status. Two months of rifampin plus pyrazinamide are not recommended because of hepatotoxicity risk, and shorter regimens should not be used when rifamycins are contraindicated. Rifampin and rifapentine are not interchangeable, and caution should be taken so that patients receive the correct medication. For patients without drug intolerability or drug-drug interactions, short-course rifamycin-based regimens are preferred over longer-course isoniazid monotherapy.
