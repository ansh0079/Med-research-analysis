## Synopsis (gist)

We recommend considering Turner syndrome in phenotypic females with one X chromosome and complete or partial absence of the second sex chromosome plus typical clinical features, and testing females who have typical signs. Growth hormone is recommended early, around 4–6 years and preferably before 12–13 years, when there is growth failure, short stature, or a strong likelihood of short stature, usually at 45–50 µg/kg/day or 1.3–1.5 mg/m2/day. Estrogen replacement should start between 11 and 12 years of age and increase to adult dosing over 2–3 years, low-dose estradiol by a systemic route is suggested with transdermal preferred, and progesterone is recommended once breakthrough bleeding occurs or after 2 years of estrogen. The extract suggests that the general surveillance guidance applies to any karyotype, although long-term echocardiographic surveillance can be omitted for those with the lowest levels of 45,X. Height is recommended to be measured at least every 4 to 6 months in the first treatment year and every 6 months thereafter, with clinical evaluation for scoliosis every 6 months during growth hormone therapy.
