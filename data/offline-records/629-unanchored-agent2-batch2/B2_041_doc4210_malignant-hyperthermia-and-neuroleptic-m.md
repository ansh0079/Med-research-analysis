# Malignant hyperthermia and neuroleptic malignant syndrome: recognition and dantrolene: evidence record (629 unanchored batch 2, not published)

**Fit:** partly

## Synopsis (gist)

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome is described as a rare but potentially fatal reaction to dopamine antagonism, and international consensus and DSM-5 research criteria require documented repeated hyperthermia for diagnosis. The review reports that rigidity and a rising creatine kinase are earlier markers than measured fever, and that a clinically important minority of diagnosed patients are afebrile at presentation. It states that early recognition should not depend on documented fever, and that withdrawal of the offending dopamine antagonist, or reinstitution of a withdrawn dopamine agonist, should not wait for the fever criterion. Aggressive supportive care aimed at hydration, renal protection, and thermoregulation is described as the cornerstone of management. Adjuncts including dantrolene, bromocriptine, benzodiazepines, and electroconvulsive therapy may be used selectively, but primary evidence is limited. The only prospective comparison cited found that dantrolene and bromocriptine, alone or combined, tended to prolong illness compared with supportive care alone.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a narrative review of early recognition of neuroleptic malignant syndrome when hyperthermia is absent or not yet documented. Malignant hyperthermia as its own syndrome, and a complete dantrolene regimen, are outside this review. Dantrolene is discussed only as a selective adjunct with limited evidence.
