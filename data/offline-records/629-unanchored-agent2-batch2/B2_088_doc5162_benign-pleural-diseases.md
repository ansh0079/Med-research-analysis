## Synopsis (gist)

This narrative review covers benign pleural diseases associated with asbestos exposure. The only cited study of rheumatoid arthritis with diffuse pleural thickening and/or interstitial fibrosis suggests those patients should be considered at increased risk for lung cancer and followed, and symptomatic patients should be considered for other techniques to exclude malignancy. For pleural plaques, the review states that in Spain a checkup with chest radiograph and forced spirometry every 1 to 3 years is recommended, with a DLCO test if spirometry is abnormal, and that follow-up may be terminated after diagnosis or offered if the patient wants to discuss future risk. For diffuse pleural thickening, an annual checkup with chest radiograph and a complete functional study including lung volumes and DLCO is recommended in the Spanish source cited, and decortication may be useful if restriction occurs without progressive pulmonary fibrosis, although it is rarely performed. If malignant disease has not manifested within 3 years from the onset of a non-malignant asbestos pleural effusion, the effusion should be considered benign, some authors recommend follow-up, and annual screening such as low-dose CT of asbestos-exposed people at increased risk of malignant disease should be considered.
