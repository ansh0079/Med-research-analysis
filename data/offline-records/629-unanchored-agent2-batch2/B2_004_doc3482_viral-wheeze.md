# viral wheeze: evidence record (629 unanchored batch 2, not published)

**Lead document:** Leukotriene receptor antagonists as maintenance and intermittent therapy for episodic viral wheeze in children (stored document id 3482, PMID 26482324). Read from the stored text.

**Topics this record serves:** viral wheeze

**Fit:** full

## Synopsis (gist)

Episodic viral wheeze associated with viral respiratory tract infections is common in pre-school children and, when wheeze occurs only with viral infections, is described as separate from atopic asthma. This Cochrane review of maintenance and intermittent leukotriene receptor antagonists in children aged one to six years included five studies of oral montelukast with 3741 participants. Maintenance treatment in a single study of the episodic viral wheeze phenotype showed no statistically significant reduction in episodes requiring rescue oral corticosteroids versus placebo, rated moderate quality evidence. Pooled intermittent treatment also showed no statistically significant reduction in episodes requiring rescue oral steroids versus placebo, rated moderate quality evidence, although one intermittent montelukast study showed a small but statistically significant reduction in unscheduled medical attendances due to wheeze. Adverse event rates were similar between treatment and placebo in the four studies that reported them. Until further data are available, these antagonists should be used with caution in individual children, and when used the authors suggest a therapeutic trial during which efficacy should be carefully monitored.
