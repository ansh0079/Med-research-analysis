## Synopsis (gist)

An audit of neonates admitted to general paediatric wards in 13 Kenyan hospitals found a dehydration prevalence of 19.7%, ranging from 9.4% to 27.0% across hospitals. Most cases with dehydration were clinically diagnosed (82 of 153), with excessive weight loss in 54 of 153 and abnormal urea, electrolytes, or creatinine in 23 of 153. Neither World Health Organization nor Kenyan guidance is described as providing explicit recommendations for diagnosis or fluid management of neonatal dehydration. Where documented, Ringers lactate and 10% dextrose were the fluids most often used, and bolus fluid was prescribed for 17 of 153 children. The limited data suggest variation in fluids, boluses, and volumes, and that volumes used may be insufficient to treat dehydration. Weight loss is often accompanied by hypernatraemia, which should be corrected gradually, and widespread lack of electrolyte testing at diagnosis and during treatment should be of concern.
