## Synopsis (gist)

Myelodysplastic neoplasms with ring sideroblasts are diagnosed via bone marrow aspiration in the presence of either at least 15% ring sideroblasts or 5–14% ring sideroblasts and an SF3B1 mutation. In the MEDALIST trial and in an interim analysis of the COMMANDS trial, lower-risk MDS with ring sideroblasts had decreased transfusion dependency with luspatercept treatment. This single-centre review found that ring sideroblasts of at least 15% by morphology alone yielded a sensitivity of only 74% for the diagnosis of MDS with SF3B1, and the study suggests that a quarter of patients would be missed by that morphologic criterion alone. The findings support the revised 2022 World Health Organization and International Consensus Classification definitions, which shift toward molecularly defined subtypes of MDS. Based on those guidelines, the authors suggest that upfront molecular testing on appropriately triaged bone marrow specimens would yield increased diagnostic accuracy for patients with MDS with SF3B1, who may eventually be eligible for luspatercept treatment. Myeloid NGS testing should not be ordered indiscriminately upfront in all patients being worked up for bone marrow disorders, but should be conducted in all patients with an established diagnosis of lower-risk MDS.
