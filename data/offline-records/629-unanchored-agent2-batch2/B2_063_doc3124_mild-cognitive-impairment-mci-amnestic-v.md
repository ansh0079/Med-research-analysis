## Synopsis (gist)

The National Institute on Aging and the Alzheimer Association workgroup set core clinical criteria for mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease for any clinical setting, and biomarker criteria intended only for research. There should be concern about a change from the prior level of cognition, lower performance in one or more cognitive domains than expected for age and education, and preserved basic daily activities, without dementia. Episodic memory impairment is the pattern most often seen in patients who later progress to Alzheimer disease dementia, although other cognitive domains can be impaired, and longitudinal assessment is important whenever possible. Positive biomarkers of both amyloid and neuronal injury give the highest likelihood that the syndrome is mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease and that progression to dementia will occur. Negative biomarkers for both amyloid and neuronal injury strongly suggest the syndrome is not due to Alzheimer disease, and a search for an alternative cause should be considered. The workgroup states that considerable work is still needed to validate the biomarker criteria and to standardize biomarker analysis for community settings.

## Hand-back notes

The source gives NIA-AA clinical and research criteria for mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease, including memory and non-memory presentations and the likelihood of progression to dementia. It does not define amnestic versus non-amnestic categories or a progression-tracking protocol that compares those subtypes.
