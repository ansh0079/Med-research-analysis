## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis compared methods to prevent contrast-associated acute kidney injury across randomised trials. The primary outcome was contrast-induced acute kidney injury, defined as at least a 25% relative increase or at least a 0.5 mg/dl increase from baseline creatinine one to five days after contrast exposure. The top ranked interventions in the main analysis were allopurinol, prostaglandin E1, and oxygen, and intravenous hydration ranked lower than those options but better than placebo. The results suggest that some options, particularly allopurinol, PGE1, and oxygen, deserve further evaluation in larger well-designed RCTs. For health care providers, the results of this meta-analysis do not suggest changes to current clinical practice. Prevention methods assessed in large studies should be evaluated case by case, with attention to comorbidities, clinical needs, prior risk factors, and national and local guidelines.
