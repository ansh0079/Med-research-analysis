## Synopsis (gist)

Thyroid nodules are estimated to occur in 36.5% of females and 23% of males, and differential diagnosis is based on fine-needle aspiration biopsies and cytological assessment. Ultrasonography with TIRADS scoring assesses nodule features, and a single enlarging nodule with irregular margins and psammoma bodies suggests malignancy and requires further fine-needle biopsy and cytology. A search through the end of June 2023 identified 19 studies applying Raman spectroscopy to the differential diagnosis of thyroid nodules, using cellular or tissue samples and serum. Papillary thyroid carcinoma detection accuracy is reported as approximately 90%, and medullary and follicular thyroid carcinoma can be detected with up to 100% accuracy, although results might be biased by low case numbers, model overfitting, and the reference method. The review states that the technique seems to have powerful application potential in thyroid tumour diagnosis and that Raman spectroscopy can be used for thyroid dysfunction detection, screening tests, and precise tumour diagnosis.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a systematic review of Raman spectroscopy as a potential adjunct for differential diagnosis of thyroid nodules. It describes fine-needle aspiration, TIRADS, and Bethesda cytology as current practice, but it does not provide a clinical guideline on FNA indications, Bethesda-category management, molecular testing, or the choice between active surveillance and surgery.
