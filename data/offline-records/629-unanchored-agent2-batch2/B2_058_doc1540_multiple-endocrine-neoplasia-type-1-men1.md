## Synopsis (gist)

This retrospective study compared 68Ga-DOTA-TATE PET/CT with contemporaneous CT or MRI in patients with MEN-1 and suspected duodeno-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours. PET/CT detected more pancreatic lesions than CT or MRI and detected duodenal lesions that were not seen on conventional CT or MRI, and a change of management was indicated in 69% of patients who had a PET/CT study. The authors suggest that somatostatin receptor-targeted PET/CT should be an integral part of staging suspected duodeno-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours in MEN-1. Once a clinical or genetic diagnosis is established, active surveillance with follow-up, biochemical testing, and imaging is recommended by cited practice guidelines, while serial PET/CT should be considered carefully because of cumulative radiation. Cited best-practice guidance suggests somatostatin-receptor PET-CT for MEN-1 patients planned for duodeno-pancreatic surgery and for surveillance when the scan would change management.
