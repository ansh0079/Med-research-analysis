# Tricuspid regurgitation: functional vs primary, transcatheter repair options: evidence record (629 unanchored batch 2, not published)

**Lead document:** Concomitant Tricuspid Repair in Patients with Degenerative Mitral Regurgitation (stored document id 4227, PMID 34767705). Read from the stored text.

**Topics this record serves:** Tricuspid regurgitation: functional vs primary, transcatheter repair options

**Fit:** partly

## Synopsis (gist)

Tricuspid regurgitation is common among patients undergoing mitral valve surgery for primary mitral regurgitation. This randomized trial compared mitral valve surgery alone with mitral valve surgery plus tricuspid annuloplasty in patients with moderate or less tricuspid regurgitation. The addition of concomitant tricuspid annuloplasty reduced the composite primary endpoint event rate at 2 years, driven by less frequent progression to severe tricuspid regurgitation. The source states that there is broad agreement that severe tricuspid regurgitation may not predictably improve after left-sided valve surgery and should be addressed at the time of the index procedure. Permanent pacemaker implantation was significantly more frequent after tricuspid annuloplasty, and that outcome should be factored into shared decision-making. At 2 years there were no differences in survival, major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events, quality of life, functional status, or readmissions, and whether less progression of tricuspid regurgitation yields long-term net benefit requires longer follow-up.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a randomized trial of concomitant surgical tricuspid annuloplasty during mitral valve surgery for primary mitral regurgitation with moderate or less tricuspid regurgitation. It does not compare functional and primary tricuspid regurgitation as separate disease entities and does not address transcatheter tricuspid repair.
