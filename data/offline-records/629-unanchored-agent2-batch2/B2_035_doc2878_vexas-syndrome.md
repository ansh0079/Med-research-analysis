## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review characterizes the phenotypic spectrum of genetically confirmed VEXAS syndrome across published cases. Cutaneous involvement was the predominant manifestation, followed by constitutional symptoms and respiratory disease, and joint involvement, ocular disease, and venous thromboembolism were also common. Myelodysplastic syndrome occurred in 35.8% of patients, and rare but clinically significant features included cardiac involvement, renal disease, and central nervous system manifestations. Given the high prevalence and early presentation of skin disease, clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion in older male patients with neutrophilic dermatoses or vasculitic lesions, particularly when accompanied by systemic inflammation or cytopenias. Corticosteroids showed high initial efficacy but limited long-term utility due to toxicity concerns, and there should be early consideration of high-dose glucocorticoid therapy when inflammatory pulmonary involvement is identified. The broad spectrum of manifestations necessitates a multidisciplinary approach, and the identified high-prevalence features should inform diagnostic strategies, particularly in older male patients presenting with these manifestations alongside cytopenias.
