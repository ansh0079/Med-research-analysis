## Synopsis (gist)

This extract is from the European consensus guideline on invasive cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, limited to diagnostics and prevention. If invasive SCC is suspected, a histopathological diagnosis shall be made, and the pathology report shall include specimen type, histological thickness or depth of invasion, grade of differentiation, perineural invasion, desmoplastic type, and margin status. It may also include histologic subtype, lymphovascular invasion, and the caliber of affected nerves when perineural invasion is at least 0.1 mm. Patients with low-risk cSCC should undergo physical examination only, with imaging only if the examination indicates it. Patients with primary common cSCC and high-risk factors should be staged for non-palpable lymph node involvement, preferably by ultrasound or CT, and CT or MRI should be used when bone or soft-tissue involvement is suspected. LacSCC should undergo imaging to rule out metastasis, and cSCC with nodal involvement should undergo a full skin examination and imaging to rule out distant metastatic disease.
