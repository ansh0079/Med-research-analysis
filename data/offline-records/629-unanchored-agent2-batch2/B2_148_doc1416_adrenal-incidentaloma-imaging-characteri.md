## Synopsis (gist)

We recommend discussing adrenal incidentalomas in a multidisciplinary expert team when imaging is not consistent with a benign lesion, and imaging every incidentaloma to see whether the mass is homogeneous and lipid-rich. Noncontrast CT is recommended as the first modality, and no further imaging is required when the mass is homogeneous with Hounsfield units of 10 or less. Every patient should be assessed for adrenal hormone excess, and a 1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test is recommended to exclude autonomous cortisol secretion, read as a continuous result, with a post-dexamethasone cortisol of 50 nmol/L (1.8 µg/dL) or less excluding autonomous cortisol secretion. We recommend against surgery for an asymptomatic, nonfunctioning unilateral mass with obvious benign imaging, and against further imaging follow-up when benign features are already clear. If an indeterminate mass is not removed, one repeat noncontrast CT or MRI after 6-12 months is suggested, with surgical resection suggested if the lesion enlarges by more than 20% and at least 5 mm, and patients with mild autonomous cortisol secretion who do not have adrenalectomy are recommended for annual reassessment of cortisol-related comorbidities.
