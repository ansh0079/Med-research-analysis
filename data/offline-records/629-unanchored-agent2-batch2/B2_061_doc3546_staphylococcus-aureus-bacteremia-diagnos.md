## Synopsis (gist)

This systematic review compared shorter versus longer antibiotic courses, with a minimum difference of 3 days, in immunocompetent patients with bloodstream infections and rated certainty with GRADE. Moderate-certainty evidence suggested little or no difference in mortality, treatment failure, and relapse, and high-certainty evidence indicated that shorter courses shorten hospital stay without increasing readmission. The authors conclude that shorter-duration and longer-duration antibiotics likely have similar effects on mortality, treatment failure, relapse, and clinical cure for bloodstream infections. They suggest that longer durations are not necessary for uncomplicated bloodstream infections, but the data should not be extrapolated to Staph aureus bacteraemia, prosthetic joint infection, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, or unresolved source control, where longer duration is the standard of care. Effects on adverse events were uncertain at very low certainty, and caution is required because subgroup data were inadequate.
