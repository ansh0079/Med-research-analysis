## Synopsis (gist)

Chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia is described as the prototype myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm and is characterized by sustained peripheral blood monocytosis. Diagnostic criteria are revised to include prerequisite and supporting criteria, and the first prerequisite criterion is persistent absolute and relative peripheral blood monocytosis. The cutoff for absolute monocytosis is lowered from 1.0 ×109/L to 0.5 ×109/L to incorporate cases formerly referred to as oligomonocytic CMML. When absolute monocytosis is at least 0.5 ×109/L but below 1.0 ×109/L, detection of a clonal cytogenetic or molecular abnormality and documentation of dysplasia in at least one lineage are required. A documented history of a myeloproliferative neoplasm excludes CMML, and MPN features or a high burden of JAK2, CALR, or MPL mutations tends to support MPN with monocytosis rather than CMML. Myelodysplastic CMML and myeloproliferative CMML are recognized by white-cell count, and the prior CMML-0 blast subgroup has been eliminated.
