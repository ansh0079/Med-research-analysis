## Synopsis (gist)

Palindromic rheumatism is described as a distinctive episodic arthropathy with recurrent, self-limiting attacks of arthritis. No controlled trials or formal treatment guidelines exist, and the review states that management is empirical. Many experts recommend antimalarials, particularly hydroxychloroquine, as first-line therapy, and these agents are described as reducing attack frequency and delaying progression to rheumatoid arthritis. Methotrexate and leflunomide are described as add-on therapies in refractory cases, and rituximab is reported for use in treatment-resistant patients. Emerging evidence suggests that iguratimod may also be effective, particularly when conventional disease-modifying drugs have not controlled attacks. The authors conclude that aggressive early treatment with antimalarials and, if needed, methotrexate or other disease-modifying drugs can achieve remission in most patients and may delay or prevent progression.
