## Synopsis (gist)

This meta-analysis compared perioperative outcomes with preoperative sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor use versus non-use in patients undergoing cardiac or non-cardiac surgery. Because of considerable heterogeneity, the primary pooled analysis found no significant association with euglycemic ketoacidosis or diabetic ketoacidosis, whereas leave-one-out sensitivity analysis showed a significant increase in both risks. Use of SGLT2 inhibitors was associated with a statistically significant decrease in both mortality and acute renal injury. According to current FDA and scientific society guidelines, SGLT2 inhibitors should be discontinued for 3 days (dapagliflozin/empagliflozin) to 4 days (ertugliflozin) before surgery. Given the minimal risk of AKI, a 3-day hold is advised for low-risk elective surgery to reduce the risk of eKA, and if strict ketone monitoring is possible a 24-h hold or continuation may be considered in high-risk cardiac/renal situations. Therefore, minimising the withholding period as short as feasible, likely 24 h or fewer under close monitoring, may be the best approach, and do not postpone emergency surgery while closely monitoring serum ketones and the anion gap.

## Hand-back notes

The document is a systematic review and meta-analysis of perioperative SGLT2 inhibitor exposure, not a general guideline for SGLT2 inhibitor therapy in type 2 diabetes. Chronic outpatient glycemic management, non-perioperative cardiovascular indications, and long-term prescribing outside the surgical period are out of scope.
