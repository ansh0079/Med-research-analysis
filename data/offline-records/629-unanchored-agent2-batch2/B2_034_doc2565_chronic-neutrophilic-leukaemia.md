## Synopsis (gist)

Chronic neutrophilic leukemia and atypical chronic myeloid leukemia are diagnosed on the basis of neoplastic expansion of granulocytic cells and exclusion of genetic drivers known in other myeloproliferative neoplasms. Activating mutations in CSF3R, the receptor for colonystimulating factor 3, were identified in 16 of 27 patients (59%) with chronic neutrophilic leukemia or atypical chronic myeloid leukemia. These data suggest that CSF3R mutations are a defining molecular abnormality of these diseases, and testing for CSF3R mutations could aid in the diagnosis. Truncation mutations are described as dysregulating SRC family–TNK2 kinases, and the data suggest they confer sensitivity to dasatinib but not to JAK kinase inhibitors, whereas membrane proximal mutations dysregulate JAK family kinases and show the reverse pattern. The two mutation classes may have differential susceptibility to tyrosine kinase inhibitors. A patient with chronic neutrophilic leukemia carrying a JAK-activating CSF3R mutation had marked clinical improvement after administration of the JAK1/2 inhibitor ruxolitinib.
