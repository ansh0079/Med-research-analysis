## Synopsis (gist)

This European Academy of Neurology guideline on the management of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis uses GRADE methodology, with strong recommendations worded as “Do”, “Don't”, or “Offer” and weak recommendations worded as “Consider”. Offer lifelong riluzole to all people with ALS at diagnosis, and the recommended dosage is 50 mg twice daily. If adverse events are noted, consider reducing the dose and re‐evaluate, and if adverse events still persist, consider stopping riluzole. Non-invasive ventilation should be offered to all patients with ALS with either symptoms, signs, or laboratory investigations supportive of respiratory insufficiency, and every effort must be made to allow the use of NIV, independently of bulbar function. Do not use diaphragmatic pacing for the treatment of ALS. In patients who do not tolerate or do not accept non-invasive or invasive ventilation, in whom non-invasive ventilation is not successful or is no longer working, or in end-stage disease, consider opioids as an option to relieve symptoms of breathlessness and consider benzodiazepines to manage breathlessness that is exacerbated by anxiety.
