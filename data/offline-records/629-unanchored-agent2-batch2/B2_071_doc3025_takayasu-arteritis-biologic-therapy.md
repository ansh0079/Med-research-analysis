## Synopsis (gist)

Takayasu's arteritis is a rare chronic arteritis that can lead to serious consequences. Understanding of the pathogenesis remains limited, and effective therapeutic strategies for this condition are lacking. Emerging data indicate that the NLRP3 inflammasome may be involved in the pathogenesis of Takayasu's arteritis, potentially contributing to the initiation of the disease. The pharmacotherapy used to treat Takayasu's arteritis includes glucocorticoids, immunosuppressive agents, and newer targeted biologic drugs such as tocilizumab and infliximab. Glucocorticoids are recommended by the American College of Rheumatology as first-line therapy. The addition of tocilizumab in patients with poor glucocorticoid response is recommended in the ACR guideline.
