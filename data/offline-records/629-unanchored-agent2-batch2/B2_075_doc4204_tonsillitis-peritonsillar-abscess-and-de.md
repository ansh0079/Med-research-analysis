## Synopsis (gist)

More than 120,000 patients are treated annually in Germany to resolve repeated episodes of acute tonsillitis. This part of the guideline covers diagnostics and nonsurgical management, and surgical management including tonsillectomy and tonsillotomy is the subject of part II. To estimate the probability of tonsillitis caused by β-hemolytic streptococci, a diagnostic scoring system according to Centor or McIsaac is suggested. If therapy is considered, a positive score of ≥3 should lead to pharyngeal swab or rapid test or culture in order to identify β-hemolytic streptococci. Routinely performed blood tests for acute tonsillitis are not indicated, and antistreptolysin O titer determination should not be performed for acute tonsillitis with or without pharyngitis. First-line therapy of β-hemolytic streptococci consists of oral penicillin, and oral intake for 7 days of one of both penicillin agents is recommended.

## Hand-back notes

This is part I of a tonsillitis guideline and covers diagnostics and nonsurgical management, including when to image suspected spread beyond the peritonsillar space. Surgical treatment of tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess drainage technique, and a full deep-neck-infection pathway are out of scope.
