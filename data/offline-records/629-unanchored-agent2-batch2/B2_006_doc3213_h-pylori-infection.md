## Synopsis (gist)

Helicobacter pylori infection is linked in this review to peptic ulcers, non-ulcer dyspepsia, recurrent peptic ulcer bleeding, unexplained iron deficiency anaemia, idiopathic thrombocytopaenia purpura, and colorectal adenomas, with confirmatory diagnosis by endoscopic biopsy and histopathology. The review compared urea breath test, serology, and stool antigen test with histopathology in 101 studies and 11,003 participants. An indirect comparison found a difference in accuracy among urea breath test-13C, urea breath test-14C, serology, and stool antigen test, and the plain-language summary states that urea breath tests were more accurate than blood and stool tests. Direct comparisons were limited, and the accuracy conclusion is based on an indirect comparison with potential confounding. In people without gastrectomy and without recent antibiotics or proton pump inhibitors, urea breath tests had high diagnostic accuracy while serology and stool antigen tests were less accurate. Urea breath tests, blood tests, and stool tests may be suitable to identify infection, but thresholds that should be used for diagnosis remain unclear.
