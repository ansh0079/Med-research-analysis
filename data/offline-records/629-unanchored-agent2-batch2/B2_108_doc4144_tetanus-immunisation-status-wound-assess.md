# Tetanus: immunisation status, wound assessment, tetanus immunoglobulin, metronidazole: evidence record (629 unanchored batch 2, not published)

**Lead document:** Chinese expert consensus on tetanus prophylaxis in major trauma (2026 Edition) (stored document id 4144, PMID 42637638). Read from the stored text.

**Topics this record serves:** Tetanus: immunisation status, wound assessment, tetanus immunoglobulin, metronidazole

**Fit:** partly

## Synopsis (gist)

Tetanus is an acute infectious disease caused by Clostridium tetani, which enters the body through contaminated wounds, proliferates under anaerobic conditions, and produces neurotoxin. This consensus forms 12 recommendations on tetanus prophylaxis in major trauma, comprising 4 strong recommendations and 8 recommendations. When the tetanus immunization history cannot be rapidly confirmed, the patient may provisionally be managed as having incomplete immunization, and a dual prophylactic strategy of active immunization plus passive immunization should be implemented if it does not interfere with emergency resuscitation. The order of preference for passive immunization preparations is TeNT-mAb, then HTIG, then F(ab')2, then TAT. Metronidazole is effective against C. tetani and can suppress bacterial proliferation and toxin production to some extent, but it cannot neutralize toxin already bound to neural tissue and cannot replace immunoprophylaxis.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a consensus on tetanus prophylaxis after major trauma. It covers immunization history, passive immunization including HTIG, and wound management in that setting. A treatment regimen for established tetanus is out of scope; metronidazole is stated only as unable to neutralize bound toxin or replace immunoprophylaxis.
