## Synopsis (gist)

This review summarizes guidelines and recent developments for treatment of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia. All patients with MRSA bacteremia should be evaluated for infective endocarditis, echocardiography should be performed, and infectious disease consultation is recommended. IDSA recommendations support vancomycin or daptomycin as first-line therapy, with uncomplicated bacteremia treated for a minimum of 2 weeks and complicated bacteremia for 4–6 weeks depending on the extent and resolution of the infection. Guidelines recommend repeating blood cultures to document clearance, and for persistent bacteremia it has been recommended that failure of cultures to clear at 3–5 days be treated as failure of monotherapy, with addition of ceftaroline to vancomycin or a change to daptomycin plus a second agent considered. Daptomycin is not recommended for pulmonary infections, and for MRSA pneumonia vancomycin or linezolid is recommended. Source control remains essential, rifampin should never be used as monotherapy, and treatment should be guided by clinical response and clearance of bacteremia.
