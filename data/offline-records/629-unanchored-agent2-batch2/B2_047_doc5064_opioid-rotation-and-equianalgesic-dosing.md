## Synopsis (gist)

This review offers practical suggestions for opioid rotation and equianalgesia, and it states that ratios are not always bidirectional. For morphine and hydromorphone, the included data suggest hydromorphone is five times more potent than morphine when morphine is given first, but only 3.7 times more potent when hydromorphone is given first, and a directional ratio of 5 versus 3.7 is reported as recommended. For fentanyl, one included study suggested that a more cautious morphine-to-fentanyl potency ratio of 100:1 may be advisable, and another suggested that 50:1 is not enough when rotating from low-dose morphine to a fentanyl patch. Product information cited in the review equates 10 mg of oral oxycodone with 20 mg of oral morphine. Expert steps described for a table-based rotation are a 25% to 50% decrease in the calculated dose of the new opioid, with stated exceptions for methadone, transdermal fentanyl, and short-acting transmucosal fentanyl, then a further 15% to 30% adjustment for the clinical context. Webster and Fine suggested decreasing the original opioid by about 10%–25% per week while slowly titrating the new opioid from a dose that would normally be used in an opioid-naive patient.

Rotation outside that evidence, including many opioid pairs with no published ratio in the source, is not covered.
