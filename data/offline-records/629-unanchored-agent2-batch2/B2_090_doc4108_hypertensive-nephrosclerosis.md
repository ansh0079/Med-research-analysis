## Synopsis (gist)

This narrative review describes hypertensive nephrosclerosis and its overlap with diabetic nephropathy as leading causes of chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal disease. Ischemic glomerular scarring with mild proteinuria in a hypertensive patient may favor hypertensive nephrosclerosis, whereas Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules, efferent arteriolar hyalinosis, long-standing diabetes, retinopathy, and heavy proteinuria support diabetic nephropathy. If active urinary sediment is present, other diagnoses should be considered, a presumptive diabetic nephropathy diagnosis may be sufficient when the course is classic, and biopsy should be considered when features are atypical. Both conditions require strict blood pressure control; the review states a diabetic nephropathy target below 130/80 mmHg with ACE inhibitors or ARBs preferred, and states that hypertensive nephrosclerosis benefits from ACE inhibitors especially when proteinuric, with other antihypertensives suitable when non-proteinuric. SGLT2 inhibitors, finerenone, and GLP-1 receptor agonists are described as advances for diabetic nephropathy and are not indicated for isolated hypertensive nephrosclerosis, while therapy should be individualized with RAAS blockade and blood pressure management, and salt restriction, weight reduction, and smoking cessation are advised.
