## Synopsis (gist)

This commentary on mesh rectopexy for rectal prolapse states that perineal approaches are associated with higher recurrence, while definitive evidence for the superiority of any specific approach remains elusive. Successful rectopexy should provide anatomical correction and also lasting benefits in continence, relief from constipation, and functional improvement. Mesh or suture fixation after rectum mobilization remains the standard practice, and a cited meta-analysis is described as demonstrating superiority of mesh rectopexy over sutured rectopexy for recurrence. A network meta-analysis is reported as showing that posterior mesh rectopexy might have a lower recurrence rate, fewer complications, and greater effects on anal incontinence than ventral mesh rectopexy and several other procedures. The authors state that small pilot studies do not provide robust evidence to guide clinical practice, and they describe mesh-related complications including erosion, infection, reoperation, chronical pain, and dyspareunia. They recommend international registries of mesh-related complications and outcomes, and they state that new mesh products mandate truly informed consent about risks, lack of long-term data, and possible industry influences.
