## Synopsis (gist)

This uncontrolled prospective study evaluated low-intensity EPOCH-R-based treatment in adults with sporadic or immunodeficiency-associated Burkitt lymphoma. With long follow-up, freedom from progression of disease and overall survival were 95% and 100% with DA-EPOCH-R and 100% and 90% with SC-EPOCH-RR, and none of the patients died from Burkitt lymphoma. Fever and neutropenia were the principal toxic events, tumor lysis syndrome developed in one patient, and no treatment-related deaths occurred. The authors conclude that low-intensity EPOCH-R-based therapy appears to obviate the need for high-intensity treatment and markedly reduces treatment toxicity while achieving high rates of durable response. The small number of patients with active central nervous system involvement precludes a confident conclusion about efficacy in that subgroup.
