## Synopsis (gist)

Acute respiratory failure is a common life-threatening complication of acute onset neuromuscular diseases and may exacerbate chronic hypoventilation in patients with neuromuscular disease or chest wall disorders. Standard management includes oxygen supplementation, physiotherapy, cough assistance and, whenever needed, antibiotics and intermittent positive pressure ventilation. Non-invasive mechanical ventilation has become routine practice in many centres, while invasive ventilation is described as the standard treatment for acute respiratory failure in this setting. This Cochrane review found no randomised trials comparing non-invasive with invasive ventilation on which to base evidence-based practice. There is no evidence from randomised studies either for or against the routine use of non-invasive ventilation instead of invasive ventilation. Some evidence from observational studies suggests that non-invasive ventilation should be trialled in all patients except for those with bulbar dysfunction, and the evidence search is current to 11 September 2017.
