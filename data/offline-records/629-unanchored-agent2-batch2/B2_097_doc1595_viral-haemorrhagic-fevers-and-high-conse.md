## Synopsis (gist)

The 2016 WHO pocket guide treats shock in viral haemorrhagic fever as hypovolaemic from gastrointestinal loss, septic, or, less often, haemorrhagic, and says intensive supportive care is the management that can be offered. For large gastrointestinal losses it uses lactated Ringer's solution, a 1 litre adult bolus over 30 minutes or faster, repeated if shock persists, with vasopressors considered if the patient does not improve. Patients with some dehydration plus weakness or vomiting are started on intravenous fluid while still being offered oral rehydration solution. The 2019 WHO Ebola supportive-care procedures add oxygen to keep saturation above 94 percent, noradrenaline as the first-line vasopressor in adults and epinephrine in children, and blood transfusion when haemoglobin is below 7 g/dl or bleeding is causing instability.
