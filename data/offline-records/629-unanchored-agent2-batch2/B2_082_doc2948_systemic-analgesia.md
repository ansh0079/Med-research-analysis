## Synopsis (gist)

This network meta-analysis compared regional analgesia techniques with systemic analgesia after radical gastrectomy for gastric cancer. Systemic analgesia was defined as intravenous patient-controlled analgesia or intravenous and/or oral opioid administration without regional analgesia. The authors conclude that regional analgesia may provide beneficial effects in reducing postoperative pain and opioid consumption after gastrectomy. Intertransverse process plane block showed favorable ranking probabilities across several postoperative pain outcomes, although these findings should be interpreted cautiously given limited certainty of evidence. The discussion states that regional analgesia is supported as a component of multimodal perioperative pain management, and that fascial plane blocks are more commonly used as opioid-sparing adjuncts rather than complete substitutes for epidural or systemic analgesia in open gastrectomy.
