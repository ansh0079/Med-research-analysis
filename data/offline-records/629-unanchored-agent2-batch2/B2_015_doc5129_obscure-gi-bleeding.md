## Synopsis (gist)

Jejunal diverticulosis is described as uncommon, and gastrointestinal bleeding from it is particularly rare and often presents as obscure gastrointestinal bleeding after negative upper and lower endoscopy. In the acute setting, the report states that CT angiography plays a central role in diagnosis and management when endoscopic evaluation is inconclusive or not feasible. Imaging in the reported case was highly suggestive of hemorrhagic jejunal diverticulosis as the source of bleeding. The authors conclude that jejunal diverticulosis is a rare but important cause of obscure gastrointestinal bleeding when initial endoscopy is negative, and that CT angiography plays a crucial role in identifying the bleeding source even without active extravasation. Conservative treatment, including fluid resuscitation and transfusion, may be sufficient in cases of self-limited bleeding. In persistent or recurrent hemorrhage, selective arterial embolization may be required, while surgical resection remains the definitive treatment in refractory cases.
