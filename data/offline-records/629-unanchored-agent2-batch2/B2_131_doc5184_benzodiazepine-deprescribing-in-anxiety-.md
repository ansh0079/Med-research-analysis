## Synopsis (gist)

This scoping review summarizes international clinical guidance on prescribing and deprescribing benzodiazepines (BZD) and benzodiazepine receptor agonists (BZRA) for anxiety disorders, mood disorders, and insomnia. Guidance has long restricted these agents to short-term use when possible, to limit acute harm from central nervous system depression and longer-term dependence, and safe deprescribing is described as a counter-balance for medication safety in an aging population. Broad consensus supports limiting prescribing to the short-term and using shared decision making with gradual dose reduction for deprescribing. Over thirty years of guidance, these agents are still clearly not recommended as routine 1st line agents or for longer-term use beyond 4 weeks in most patients. Particular tapering regimens, switching to a long-acting BZD, adjunctive or substitutive non-BZD pharmacotherapy, and micro-tapering were inconsistently or insufficiently addressed, while cognitive-behavioural therapy is acknowledged to increase the success of gradual dose reduction for discontinuation in both insomnia and anxiety disorders.
