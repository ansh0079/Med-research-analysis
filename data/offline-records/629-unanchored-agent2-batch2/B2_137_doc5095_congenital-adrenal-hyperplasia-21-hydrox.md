## Synopsis (gist)

This review discusses obesity in classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, including mechanisms, complications, and management. It states that a growing body of research suggests obesity in patients with classic CAH may commence early in life, and that obesity-related complications can affect insulin function, lipid profiles, blood pressure, and other organ systems. Because a non-dipping blood-pressure pattern is described as highly prevalent, 24-h ambulatory monitoring should be considered if office readings are elevated or if there is target-organ damage, and in adults a target below 130/80 mmHg is recommended. Fasting plasma glucose and/or HbA1c should be assessed annually in all adults with classic CAH regardless of BMI, and screening for dysglycemia should start by 10 years of age in children with overweight or obesity, or earlier if insulin resistance is clinically evident. Care is described as multidisciplinary, with an endocrinologist overseeing glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid titration, and surgical options in patients with obesity should be chosen with caution.
