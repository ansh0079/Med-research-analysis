## Synopsis (gist)

This review defines metabolic acidosis in chronic kidney disease by a serum bicarbonate concentration lower than 22 mmol/L, and uses pH and the anion gap to judge the depth and type of the disorder. In patients with chronic kidney disease and coexisting COPD, the degree of compensation for metabolic acidosis associated with decreased pCO2 should be assessed. According to current guidelines cited in the review, SGLT2i can be safely used when eGFR is at least 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, but close monitoring of pH, bicarbonate, and ketone bodies is recommended, especially during increased metabolic stress. Unexplained hyperkalemic metabolic acidosis with a normal anion gap should lead clinicians to consider distal or proximal renal tubular acidosis, after blood pH confirms metabolic acidosis rather than compensation for respiratory alkalosis, and then urine pH and ammonia excretion. The review states that the main treatments discussed are bicarbonates and veverimer, together with a diet rich in vegetables and fruits, while also stating that there is still insufficient evidence that correction of metabolic acidosis benefits patients.
