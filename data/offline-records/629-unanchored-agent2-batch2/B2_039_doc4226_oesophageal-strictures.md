## Synopsis (gist)

Inherited epidermolysis bullosa commonly involves the gastrointestinal tract, and oesophageal strictures are among the most disabling complications, with progressive dysphagia contributing to malnutrition, chronic anaemia and growth delay. This GRADE clinical practice guideline recommends radiological investigations to diagnose oesophageal strictures and their characteristics in patients with suggestive signs and symptoms, and recommends against endoscopic examination for diagnosis. It recommends prompt detection, and conditionally recommends considering nonpharmacological measures and pharmacological measures for preventing or delaying onset or progression. Gastro-oesophageal reflux should be considered and treated according to current guidelines, and typical symptoms may respond to lifestyle and dietary modifications alone or, when ineffective, combined with proton pump inhibitors as first-line treatment. Oesophageal dilatation, with gastrostomy whenever needed, is recommended for management, bougie dilatation is recommended against, and fluoroscopically guided balloon dilatation is conditionally recommended as the first-line option. Oesophageal dilatations are recommended for recurrence, and patients with relapse should be referred to an expert centre for re-evaluation and planning of dilatation and follow-up.

## Hand-back notes

The source is a clinical practice guideline limited to oesophageal strictures in inherited epidermolysis bullosa. Peptic, caustic, malignant, and other non-epidermolysis bullosa oesophageal strictures are out of scope.
