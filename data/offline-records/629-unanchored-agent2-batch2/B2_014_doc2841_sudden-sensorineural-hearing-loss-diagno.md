## Synopsis (gist)

Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is described as a rapid hearing decline, often accompanied by tinnitus and vertigo. This systematic review and meta-analysis compared defibrinogenation therapy, such as batroxobin, with controls who did not receive defibrinogenation therapy. Defibrinogenation therapy was not statistically significant for improving hearing when compared with controls, and the findings suggest that defibrinogenation treatment is not more effective than other treatments for sudden sensorineural hearing loss. The authors conclude that defibrinogenation treatment is not more effective than other alternative modalities and is comparable to the spontaneous recovery rate. Based on the currently available evidence, its routine clinical use cannot be recommended as a superior therapeutic strategy over existing standard care. Clinicians should consider these findings in the context of individual patient characteristics when determining the most appropriate treatment modality.
