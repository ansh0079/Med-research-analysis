**Fit:** full

## Synopsis (gist)

Lupus nephritis is present in over 50% of patients with systemic lupus erythematosus and is managed with immunosuppressive and immunomodulatory therapies. Treatment is in general subdivided into induction and maintenance therapy. This review of meta-analyses found that calcineurin inhibitors, particularly tacrolimus, in combination with glucocorticoids outperformed cyclophosphamide with glucocorticoids in the rate of remission, either complete or partial remission, and in terms of infectious complications. Tacrolimus may be considered as another non-CPA alternative for induction therapy and in refractory disease or intolerance to cyclophosphamide or mycophenolate mofetil, and tacrolimus with glucocorticoids or multitarget therapy are described as particularly effective at inducing remission without increases in infection or malignancy. In maintenance therapy, mycophenolate mofetil was superior to azathioprine because mycophenolate mofetil-treated patients had a lower relapse rate, and for maintenance after remission mycophenolate mofetil and glucocorticoids are described as reducing mortality, infection, progression to end stage kidney disease, and relapse. Mycophenolate mofetil has a black box warning of embryo-fetal toxicity and thus should not be used in pregnant patients and patients who plan to conceive (men and women), and adverse effects should be considered to guide selection of therapy.
