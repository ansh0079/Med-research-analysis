Background Bronchial artery embolization (BAE) is widely used for hemoptysis, but the supporting evidence has mainly been derived from observational studies, and its certainty remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of BAE compared with other treatment modalities based exclusively on randomized controlled trials (RCTs), with evidence certainty assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach. Methods We searched seven databases from inception to December 29, 2024, without language restrictions. Two reviewers independently screened studies, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias. Random-effects meta-analyses were performed, and evidence certainty was assessed using GRADE. Comparator-specific analyses were primary for the main outcomes, while pooled analyses across different comparators were exploratory. An updated search on June 24, 2026 identified no additional eligible RCTs. The review was registered in PROSPERO. Result Twenty RCTs involving 1,594 patients were included. Compared with pharmacotherapy, BAE showed higher clinical efficacy (RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.18–1.31), lower recurrence (RR 0.26, 95% CI 0.13–0.54), lower mortality (RR 0.20, 95% CI 0.05–0.90), and fewer overall complications (RR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26–0.63), all supported by moderate-certainty evidence. BAE plus usual care versus usual care showed higher clinical efficacy (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.12–1.43; very-low-certainty evidence) and lower recurrence (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.15–0.53; moderate-certainty evidence), but no clear differences in mortality (RR 0.36, 95% CI 0.05–2.43) or overall complications (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.45–2.39), both with low-certainty evidence. Compared with bronchoscopic balloon tamponade, BAE showed no clear difference in clinical efficacy (RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.80–1.03; low-certainty evidence), recurrence (RR 2.14, 95% CI 0.17–27.10; very-low-certainty evidence), or overall complications (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.54–2.46; low-certainty evidence). Conclusions The comparative effects of BAE varied by treatment strategy. Evidence was more consistent versus pharmacotherapy, while estimates for bronchoscopic balloon tamponade and add-on usual-care designs were less certain. Findings should therefore be interpreted by specific treatment contrast rather than as a single overall effect. Further well-designed multicenter RCTs are needed. Of the 20 included studies, 12 studies (60%) reported the method of random sequence generation, 1 (5%) implemented allocation concealment, and all 20 (100%) ensured blinding of data collectors and outcome assessors. Blinding of surgeons and patients was not feasible. However, we judged that the lack of blinding was unlikely to exert a substantial impact on the assessed outcomes. The risk of bias attributable to incomplete outcome data and selective reporting was assessed as low across all studies, whereas funding sources were only reported in five studies and remained unclear in the remaining studies. The results from the quality assessment using the Risk of Bias (RoB 2) are summarized in ( Supplementary Material eTable 1 ). 3.4 Clinical efficacy Clinical efficacy was reported in 17 RCTs ( 24 – 33 , 35 , 37 – 42 ) involving 1,345 patients. In the primary comparator-specific analyses, moderate-certainty evidence showed higher clinical efficacy with BAE than with pharmacotherapy (RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.18–1.31). Very-low-certainty evidence supported higher clinical efficacy with BAE plus usual care versus usual care (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.12–1.43), whereas low-certainty evidence showed no clear difference between BAE and bronchoscopic balloon tamponade (RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.80–1.03). Effect estimates differed across comparator types (P for between-group differences <0.001; Figure 2 ; Table 2 ). In the primary comparator-specific analyses, the RR was 0.26 (95% CI 0.13–0.54) for BAE versus pharmacotherapy, 0.28 (95% CI 0.15–0.53) for BAE plus usual care versus usual care, and 2.14 (95% CI 0.17–27.10) for BAE versus bronchoscopic balloon tamponade. No significant difference in effect estimates was detected across comparator types ( P = 0.29; Figure 3 ). In an exploratory secondary analysis pooling the different comparator strategies, the overall RR was 0.38 (95% CI 0.21–0.69; Supplementary Materials eFigure 3 ). 3.6 Overall complications and adverse events Overall complications were reported in 13 RCTs ( 6 , 24 , 26 – 31 , 33 – 35 , 40 , 42 ) involving 924 patients. In the primary comparator-specific analyses, moderate-certainty evidence showed a lower risk of overall complications with BAE versus pharmacotherapy (RR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26–0.63), whereas low-certainty evidence showed no clear difference for BAE plus usual care versus usual care (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.45–2.39) or for BAE versus bronchoscopic balloon tamponade (RR 1.15, 95% CI 0.54–2.46). Effect estimates differed across comparator types (P for between-group differences =0.02; Figure 4 ; Table 2 ). In an exploratory secondary analysis pooling the different comparator strategies, the overall RR was 0.63 (95% CI 0.44–0.91; Supplementary Materials eFigure 4 ). Mortality was reported in 5 RCTs ( 6 , 24 , 40 – 42 ) involving 356 patients. In the primary comparator-specific analyses, moderate-certainty evidence showed lower mortality with BAE versus pharmacotherapy (RR 0.20, 95% CI 0.05–0.90), whereas low-certainty evidence showed no clear difference for BAE plus usual care versus usual care (RR 0.36, 95% CI 0.05–2.43). No significant difference in effect estimates was detected between treatment contrasts ( P = 0.64; Figure 5 ; Table 2 ). In an exploratory secondary analysis pooling the different comparator strategies, the overall RR was 0.25 (95% CI 0.08–0.81; Supplementary materials eFigure 5 ). Moderate-certainty evidence also suggested lower risks of nausea and vomiting (5 RCTs ( 30 , 33 , 34 , 40 , 42 ), 348 patients; RR 0.11, 95% CI 0.03–0.40; Supplementary Materials eFigure 6 ) and abdominal pain (3 RCTs ( 34 , 40 , 42 ), 200 patients; RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.05–1.67). The effect of BAE on chest pain (9 RCTs ( 26 – 31 , 35 , 40 , 42 ) and 641 patients; RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.23–1.23; Supplementary Materials eFigure 7 ), and palpitations (4 RCTs ( 27 , 35 , 40 , 42 ) and 304 patients; RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.18–2.15) were supported by moderate certainty evidence. The effect on cough (3 RCTs ( 29 , 40 , 42 ) and 200 patients; RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.05–15.51) was supported by low certainty evidence. However, the moderate evidence of fever (7 RCTs ( 27 – 31 , 34 , 35 ) and 482 patients; RR 1.28, 95% CI 0.48–3.40; Supplementary Materials eFigure 8 ) and the low evidence of chest distress (2 RCTs ( 26 , 33 ) and 170 patients; RR 1.23, 95% CI 0.34–4.48) indicated higher incidence of these adverse reactions in BAE treatment compared with the control group ( Supplementary eTable 4 ). 3.7 Additional outcomes Low certainty evidence from 5 RCTs ( 28 , 32 , 36 , 40 , 42 ) encompassing 415 patients showed that post-treatment pulmonary function in the BAE group was superior to that in the control group (FEV1/FVC, WMD 7.62, 95% CI 4.80–10.45; Supplementary Materials eFigure 9 ). The effect of BAE on arterial blood gas parameters including PaO2 (4 RCTs ( 32 , 36 , 40 , 42 ) and 337 patients; WMD 1.38, 95% CI 0.48–2.28), PaCO2 (4 RCTs ( 32 , 36 , 40 , 42 ) and 337 patients; WMD −1.00, 95% CI −1.68 to −0.32), and pH (4 RCTs ( 32 , 36 , 40 , 42 ) and 337 patients; WMD 0.11, 95% CI −0.06–0.29), was supported by only low certainty evidence ( Supplementary eTable 5 ). 3.8 Subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses Subgroup analyses according to follow-up time are presented in the Supplementary Materials . Comparator-specific analyses of the major outcomes showed differences in effect estimates across comparator types for clinical efficacy and overall complications, but not for recurrence or mortality ( Supplementary Materials eTables 2–S3; eFigure 1; eFigures 3–S5 ). Sensitivity analyses using the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman method yielded effect estimates of similar direction and magnitude to those obtained with the DerSimonian–Laird method. However, for some outcomes (e.g., overall complications, PaO₂, and PaCO₂), the confidence intervals became wider and crossed the null value, indicating reduced statistical certainty ( Supplementary Materials eTables 6 to 7 ). Sensitivity analyses excluding trials at high risk of bias were additionally performed for the main outcomes. For several treatment contrasts, the effect estimates were similar after exclusion of high-risk trials, whereas for others the estimates changed materially or only one lower-risk trial remained. These analyses informed the GRADE risk-of-bias judgments and are presented in Supplementary eFigures 10–S13 and eTable 9 . Because the exploratory overall analyses combined clinically distinct comparator strategies, these pooled estimates should not be interpreted as uniform treatment effects or used to guide decisions for specific clinical comparisons. 4 Discussion 4.1 Principal findings To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to synthesize evidence exclusively from randomized controlled trials evaluating bronchial artery embolization (BAE) for hemoptysis. By minimizing the influence of confounding inherent in observational studies and evaluating evidence certainty using the GRADE approach, this review provides a more rigorous assessment of the comparative efficacy and safety of BAE. In this meta-analysis of 20 RCTs involving 1,594 patients, the primary comparator-specific analyses showed that the comparative effects of BAE varied according to the treatment contrast. Compared with pharmacotherapy, BAE was associated with higher clinical efficacy, lower recurrence and mortality, and fewer overall complications. BAE plus usual care was associated with higher clinical efficacy and lower recurrence than usual care alone, whereas no clear differences were observed for mortality or overall complications. Comparisons between BAE and bronchoscopic balloon tamponade did not show clear differences for several major outcomes, although the available evidence was limited. The certainty of evidence varied across outcomes and treatment contrasts. For clinical efficacy, variation in the definitions and assessment criteria across trials introduced clinical heterogeneity and indirectness in outcome measurement, warranting a one-level downgrade in the certainty of evidence. The between-comparator differences observed for clinical efficacy and overall complications further support interpretation within specific treatment contrasts rather than reliance on a single overall pooled estimate. In contrast, no significant between-comparator differences were detected for recurrence or mortality, although the number of studies was limited for several treatment contrasts. Overall pooled analyses across the different comparator strategies were considered exploratory and should not be interpreted as uniform treatment effects applicable to specific clinical comparisons. In the exploratory overall analysis, fewer overall complications were observed with BAE; however, sensitivity analysis using the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman method produced wider confidence intervals, and statistical significance was not retained. This finding should therefore be interpreted cautiously. The favorable effects observed in several comparator-specific analyses are biologically plausible. Clinically significant hemoptysis usually originates from hypertrophied bronchial arteries and abnormal systemic collateral vessels resulting from chronic pulmonary inflammation ( 43 , 44 ). By selectively embolizing these pathological vessels, BAE directly eliminates the bleeding source and interrupts persistent collateral circulation, thereby improving immediate hemostasis and reducing the risk of recurrent bleeding. Advances in endovascular techniques, including superselective microcatheterization, more comprehensive embolization of collateral vessels, and the increasing use of permanent embolic materials, may have further contributed to improved long-term outcomes. No significant difference was observed in time to hemostasis. This finding was supported by very-low-certainty evidence based on a limited number of studies. Variations in the definition and assessment of hemostasis across studies may have further increased uncertainty. Because most included trials enrolled patients with moderate-to-severe or massive hemoptysis, the applicability of these findings to patients with mild or intermittent bleeding remains uncertain. 4.2 Relation to previous studies To date, five English-language systematic reviews related to BAE for hemoptysis have been published ( 9 – 13 ). The most recent review, published in 2024, included three RCTs together with nine cohort studies ( 11 ), whereas the remaining reviews were based exclusively on observational evidence. In contrast, the present review synthesized evidence solely from randomized controlled trials and incorporated 19 additional RCTs that had not been evaluated previously. This discrepancy is likely attributable primarily to differences in database coverage, as our search included four major Chinese databases (CNKI, Wanfang, CQVIP, and SinoMed) in addition to international databases. Nevertheless, the predominance of Chinese-language RCTs and the methodological reporting limitations of some of these studies should be considered when interpreting the findings. Our comparator-specific findings are broadly consistent with previous evidence suggesting beneficial effects of BAE for hemoptysis. Compared with earlier reports, recurrence following BAE appears to have declined over time, likely reflecting advances in interventional practice, including routine preprocedural CT angiography, superselective microcatheterization, more complete embolization of non-bronchial systemic collateral vessels, and wider adoption of permanent embolic agents. Concurrent improvements in disease-specific medical therapy, such as standardized anti-tuberculosis and antifungal treatment, may also contribute to improved long-term outcomes. The effectiveness of BAE should also be interpreted in the context of the underlying etiology of hemoptysis. Patients included in the present review had diverse causes of bleeding, including bronchiectasis, tuberculosis, malignancy, and other pulmonary diseases ( 45 ). Because patients with different etiologies of hemoptysis exhibit distinct patterns of vascular remodeling and collateral vessel formation, the magnitude and durability of treatment benefit may vary across disease populations. Future randomized studies should therefore evaluate treatment effects according to underlying disease etiology. From a clinical perspective, our findings further support current guideline recommendations that BAE should be considered an early therapeutic option for patients with moderate-to-massive hemoptysis, recurrent bleeding, or those unsuitable for surgery. Surgery remains appropriate for selected patients with localized resectable lesions, whereas bronchoscopy continues to play an essential role in airway protection, bleeding localization, and etiological diagnosis. These approaches should therefore be regarded as complementary components of multidisciplinary hemoptysis management rather than competing treatment strategies ( 46 ). The comparator interventions represented distinct therapeutic strategies, including pharmacotherapy, bronchoscopic balloon tamponade, surgery, and usual care in add-on treatment designs. Accordingly, comparator-specific analyses were used as the primary basis for interpretation, whereas overall pooled analyses across these heterogeneous strategies were considered exploratory. Compared with pharmacotherapy, BAE was associated with favorable effects across several major outcomes, whereas evidence from comparisons with bronchoscopic balloon tamponade and surgery was more limited. The add-on comparisons of BAE plus usual care versus usual care provided evidence on the incremental effect of BAE when added to background treatment. Further direct randomized comparisons between BAE and alternative interventional strategies would help define their relative roles in the management of hemoptysis. 4.3 Strengths, limitations, and future perspectives The principal strength of this review is the exclusive inclusion of randomized controlled trials, thereby reducing the influence of selection bias and confounding inherent in observational studies. In addition, methodological quality and evidence certainty were systematically evaluated using the RoB 2 tool, sensitivity analyses, and the GRADE approach. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although only randomized trials were included, some studies had methodological limitations, particularly inadequate reporting of randomization procedures, allocation concealment, and blinding, which may have affected the reliability of several outcome estimates. These methodological limitations, together with the contribution of high-risk-of-bias trials and the results of sensitivity analyses excluding such trials, were considered in the GRADE risk-of-bias judgments. In addition, although peer-review procedures could be independently verified for the journals publishing half of the included studies, such verification was unavailable for the remaining studies, which should be considered when interpreting the evidence base. Second, most included studies were conducted in China, where tuberculosis and post-tuberculosis sequelae remain common causes of hemoptysis. Consequently, the geographical and etiological distribution of the available randomized evidence may not fully reflect that of other regions, particularly settings in which malignancy or other causes account for a greater proportion of hemoptysis. Differences in disease spectrum, healthcare systems, and clinical practice may therefore limit the generalizability of the effect estimates to such populations. Nevertheless, the included trials were not restricted to a single etiology, and the fundamental therapeutic mechanism of BAE—occlusion of the culprit systemic arteries to control bleeding—is not expected to differ substantially according to geographical setting or underlying etiology. Therefore, while caution is warranted when extrapolating the magnitude of the observed effects to populations with substantially different etiological profiles, further geographically and etiologically diverse RCTs are needed to strengthen the external validity of the evidence. These concerns were considered primarily in relation to indirectness and generalizability rather than risk of bias. Third, although exploratory overall analyses were retained for completeness, they combined clinically heterogeneous comparator strategies and therefore should not be interpreted as estimates directly applicable to any single treatment comparison. In addition, the number of trials was limited for some treatment contrasts, particularly comparisons with bronchoscopic balloon tamponade and surgery, resulting in less precise comparator-specific estimates. Fourth, insufficient reporting prevented subgroup analyses according to disease etiology, embolic materials, and embolization techniques, limiting exploration of potential treatment-effect modifiers. Fifth, sensitivity analyses using the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman method yielded wider confidence intervals than the DerSimonian–Laird approach, with the confidence intervals including the null for some secondary outcomes. The certainty of evidence varied across outcomes and treatment contrasts, with more consistent evidence for comparisons with pharmacotherapy and lower or very-low-certainty evidence for several other comparisons. Future multicenter randomized trials with standardized outcome definitions, longer follow-up, and stratification according to disease etiology are needed to strengthen the evidence base and determine the optimal embolization strategies for different patient populations. 5 Conclusions In conclusion, this systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs showed that the comparative effects of bronchial artery embolization for hemoptysis varied across treatment strategies. Comparator-specific analyses showed more consistent evidence of benefit when BAE was compared with pharmacotherapy, whereas evidence for add-on usual-care designs and bronchoscopic balloon tamponade was less certain for several outcomes. Overall pooled estimates across heterogeneous comparator strategies should be regarded as exploratory rather than as uniform treatment effects applicable to specific clinical comparisons. Further well-designed multicenter randomized controlled trials with standardized outcome measures and longer follow-up are needed to evaluate the comparative efficacy and safety of BAE across diverse patient populations.
